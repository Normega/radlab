# Add reversed items, then model them

Yes. Pool 6 should add reverse-keyed items, but the reason is narrower than the textbook one, and the items need rules that limit their known costs. The decisive argument comes from the RCT, not the EFA. **Acquiescence cannot be separated from content on an all-positive scale**: every statistical correction needs some items keyed the opposite way ([Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf)). A course that teaches the items' own "sensing mode / doing mode" vocabulary is exactly the setting in which agreement can rise while the construct stays put. Meditators endorse positively worded mindfulness items more than non-meditators who have the same trait level ([Van Dam et al. 2009](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1220506-differential-item-function-across-meditators-and-non-meditators-on-the-five-facet-mindfulness-questionnaire)). The costs are real and better documented than the benefits. Mixed-key forms lose about **.04–.07 of alpha**. About **10% inconsistent respondents is enough to produce a spurious "reversed-item" factor**. And the lab's 6-point no-midpoint format is where misresponse was highest in the one experiment that varied format: **57–65% of respondents, against 10–38% on scales with a midpoint**. That figure is an upper bound, because of how misresponse was scored. The recommendation is about **14 reversed items, roughly 30% of a ~48-item pool**: one in every facet except Drift awareness, plus a second in the largest facets. Each should describe the presence of an opposite stance, be a true contradictory of a named partner item, contain no negations, and sit well away from its partner. Analyse them on unrecoded ordinal data, with a random-intercept acquiescence factor in both EFA and CFA. Use inconsistency to flag respondents, not to exclude them. Test invariance across time and arm, and report the RCT effect separately for positively and reverse-keyed items. If the reversals fail in Study 1, keep the best 4–6 in the RCT anyway, unscored, so that acquiescence stays estimable. The evidence is thin in three places that matter here. No study has measured acquiescence bias in an RCT treatment effect. No simulation has tested the random-intercept model below one-third reversed items. And no experiment has tested true antonyms in a 6-point format on psychological items.

## Mixing keys costs reliability and manufactures a wording factor

Randomized and within-person experiments that hold content fixed agree on the main cost: **switching direction, not reversed wording itself, is what degrades a scale**. Suárez-Álvarez and colleagues had 374 adults complete regular, reversed and combined forms of the same 20-item self-efficacy test. Alpha was **.932 regular, .921 reversed and .879 combined**. Explained variance fell from 48% to 36%, and mean IRT discrimination fell from 1.86 to 1.36 ([Suárez-Álvarez et al. 2018](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4463.pdf)). In a four-arm randomization of the Need for Cognition scale (N = 1,266), the mixed original fit a single factor badly (CFI .70, α .88). The all-positive version and an all-reversed version built from polar opposites both reached CFI .89 and α .94–.95 ([Zhang, Noor & Savalei 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4909292/)). A 4-point burnout scale with no midpoint lost the most. Reliability was **.65–.73 mixed against .92–.93 for either pure form**, and the general factor's explained common variance (ECV) fell to .25–.41 ([Zeng, Jeon & Wen 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11486723/)). Construction matters as much as mixing. In a three-arm study, five antonym reversals cost almost nothing in ω (.855 against .861 all-positive), while five negations cost .045 (.816). Both mixed forms still came out two-dimensional ([Ilhan et al. 2024](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440235.pdf)).

The second cost hits the EFA directly. **A small minority of inconsistent respondents is enough to create a factor made of reversed items.** About 10% careless respondents generated "a clearly definable negative factor" ([Schmitt & Stults 1985](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/84818ada-669f-4a82-a5dd-556e31cb25f6)). The same 10% led simulations to reject a true one-factor model ([Woods 2006](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s10862-005-9004-7)). Across adult and school samples, factor-mixture models classify **7–20%** of respondents as inconsistent ([Steinmann, Strietholt & Braeken 2022](https://uv.uio.no/cemo/om/aktuelt/publikasjoner/2021/a-constrained-factor-mixture-analysis-model-for-co.html)) and 4.4–10% as careless. Removing those respondents made the wording factors "practically disappear" ([Arias et al. 2020](https://produccioncientifica.usal.es/documentos/60055ebb68c34960007088fc)). Prolific's attention-check pass rates are better than MTurk's or a SONA pool's ([Douglas, Ewell & Brauer 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0279720.html)). Even so, a Prolific EFA of ~500 should expect to cross the 10% threshold. The factor that results is real variance, but its correlates are response-process variables: **low reading comprehension and reasoning predict it more strongly than any trait** ([Gnambs & Schroeders 2020](https://kobra.uni-kassel.de/handle/123456789/12439); [Steinmann 2024](https://www.iea.nl/sites/default/files/2024-04/To-Mix-or-Not-to-Mix-Positively-and-Negatively-Worded-Items.pdf)). The weight of evidence treats it as method, to be modelled, not as a facet to be named.

The literature's most direct warning matches the lab's first instinct, which was "a subset" of reversals. Weijters, Baumgartner and Schillewaert advise against unbalanced scales in which **"a few reversed items are included among many regular items"**, and against placing a reversal after a long run of regular items ([Weijters, Baumgartner & Schillewaert 2013](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/4099670/6801377)). The reason is that a minority of reversals brings in the inconsistency problem while cancelling only part of the acquiescence. The most thorough applied review recommends only positively worded items, especially for lower-ability populations ([Steinmann 2024](https://www.iea.nl/sites/default/files/2024-04/To-Mix-or-Not-to-Mix-Positively-and-Negatively-Worded-Items.pdf)). Survey methodologists list three objections to balancing an agree–disagree scale: true opposites are hard to write, negations hurt comprehension, and balancing adds a method factor ([Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)). A 2025 review concludes that clear guidelines "cannot be provided" yet ([Elek et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)).

These costs are large in the factor solution and modest in the trait estimate. That difference matters for the decision. In a meta-analytic SEM of the Rosenberg scale (113 samples, N = 140,671), positive- and negative-wording factors accounted for **less than 15% of explained common variance** ([Gnambs, Scharl & Schroeders 2018](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/metaSEM/html/Gnambs18.html)). In Weijters et al.'s integrative model, method variance from acquiescence, carelessness and inconsistency was .13–.14 relative to substantive variance, so **the trait contributed 7–8 times more variance** ([Weijters, Baumgartner & Schillewaert 2013](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/4099670/6801377)). Wording effects mislead dimensionality decisions far more than they distort scores. A pre-specified model corrects dimensionality decisions. Nothing corrects distorted scores after the fact. The same authors add the counterpoint that frames the rest of this report: removing reversed items does not remove these biases. It leaves them **"completely confounded with content variance."**

## The 6-point no-midpoint format is the worst cell for misresponse

The research notes disagree on one point, and the disagreement can be settled. The item-writing notes could not retrieve the direction of the Weijters, Cabooter and Schillewaert (2010) format effects. They recorded only an unverified recollection, that misresponse fell with "more categories and/or full labelling". Two other sets of notes read the working paper's full text: the psychometric-costs notes, and the response-style notes from the earlier midpoint review. Both report the same direction and the same figures, so the full-text reading stands. The experiment randomized 1,207 Belgian online-panel men to 4, 5, 6 or 7 points, crossed with full versus endpoint-only labels. Misresponse was scored as answering on the same side of the scale to both items of a reversed pair ([Weijters, Cabooter & Schillewaert 2010](https://public.vlerick.com/Publications/74eb09c8-6aa9-e011-8a89-005056a635ed.pdf)).

| Labelling | 4-point | 5-point | 6-point | 7-point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All points labelled | 61.8% | 10.5% | **56.6%** | 14.9% |
| Endpoints only | 57.3% | 22.8% | 64.5% | 38.4% |

*Percentage of respondents misresponding to a reversed pair, averaged over three pairs (working-paper Table 3).*

The driver is the midpoint, not the number of categories. **Adding a midpoint lowers misresponse, and lowers it significantly more when every point is labelled** (Wald χ² = 13.31, p = .0003). Full labelling also lowers misresponse. Extra gradations raise it only when just the endpoints are labelled. The recollection in the item-writing notes was therefore half right: full labelling does help, but more categories do not. With full labels, 6 points (56.6%) did slightly better than 4 (61.8%), and 7 slightly worse than 5. The authors' advice is to "avoid scales without a midpoint, unless particular, relevant reasons present themselves", and they say even-numbered formats "perform poorly in terms of MR" ([Weijters, Cabooter & Schillewaert 2010](https://public.vlerick.com/Publications/74eb09c8-6aa9-e011-8a89-005056a635ed.pdf)). Three caveats keep the 6-point figure from being read as a prevalence estimate. First, the scoring rule cannot count a midpoint answer as misresponse, so on an even scale a genuinely ambivalent person is mechanically counted as inconsistent. Second, the pairs were loose brand opposites ("I love this brand" / "I find this a very bad brand"). Third, the published IJRM version was never compared against the working paper. **Treat 57% as an upper bound for this format, not a forecast.**

One detail of that experiment matters more for pool 6 than the headline rate. **Misresponse was overwhelmingly "disagree with both" (39% of respondents) rather than "agree with both" (2%).** That is not acquiescence. It is what happens when ambivalent respondents denied a midpoint lean negative, which is Weijters's own account. It is also what the logical-response account predicts for loose opposites. Kam, Meyer and Sun find that agreeing or disagreeing with both an item and its reversal peaks **at mid-trait levels**, where doing so is logically defensible ([Elek et al. 2025, summarizing Kam et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12581211/)). An average-height person should disagree with both "I am tall" and "I am short". In a no-midpoint format, then, the main threat from reversals is not yea-saying. It is **contrary pairs that leave mid-trait respondents nowhere consistent to stand**. That turns the drafting rule "write contradictories, not contraries" from a stylistic preference into the main defence. It also means the lab should check whether "Prefer not to answer" is acting as a hidden midpoint, with refusals concentrating on reversed items. No study has tested this, and the notes found nothing on whether a per-item decline option changes misresponse at all.

This interacts with the format decision the lab has already made. The earlier midpoint review named "does the scale use reverse-keyed items?" as one of five questions that should drive the midpoint choice, and found that reversals favour a midpoint. Adding reversals tips that question toward 7 points. It does not overturn the decision. The review's stronger conclusion was that one instrument should keep one format across all its studies, and the reliability evidence shows little scale-level cost for an even format. The practical response is to keep the 6-point fully labelled format, which is the less damaging of the two even-scale cells. Write reversals that a mid-trait respondent can answer consistently. And pre-specify a model that absorbs what misresponse remains.

## Contemplative training changes how people read reversed items

The mindfulness literature adds a cost that general psychometrics does not: **reversed items in this domain behave differently in people who have trained**. In the FFMQ, keying is almost fully confounded with facet, because Acting with Awareness and Non-judging are written negatively. Meditators and non-meditators with the same latent mindfulness showed differential item functioning on **18 of 39 items**. Meditators were more likely to endorse the positively worded items, and non-meditators more likely to deny the negatively worded ones ([Van Dam, Earleywine & Danoff-Burg 2009](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1220506-differential-item-function-across-meditators-and-non-meditators-on-the-five-facet-mindfulness-questionnaire); 18/39 figure via [a secondary summary](https://pucrs.emnuvens.com.br/revistapsico/article/view/27229)). Positive and negative wording formed substantive method effects, and the authors advised against an FFMQ total score ([Van Dam et al. 2012](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/mind-your-words-positive-and-negative-items-create-method-effects/)). Across ACT-based treatment in 2,928 patients, **strong invariance held for four FFMQ facets but failed for Acting with Awareness**, the reverse-keyed "autopilot" facet. The authors framed the failure as response shift ([Levin-Aspenson et al. 2023](https://contextualscience.org/publications/levinaspenson_marks_dalrymple_zimmerman_2023)). In bifactor models, Acting with Awareness also lacked adequate reliability once the general factor was partialled out ([Aguado et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00404/full)). A Japanese online FFMQ short form fit only after positive- and negative-wording method factors were added ([Takahashi et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.833381/full)).

The MAAS shows the underlying mechanism, which bears directly on the Drift awareness facet. The MAAS scores mindfulness as reverse-scored reports of autopilot. Its IRT analysis found **limited ability to report on mindlessness and a response bias to "mindfulness-absent" items**, and concluded that reverse-scoring "may be inadequate to represent intentional attention or awareness" ([Van Dam, Earleywine & Borders 2010](https://sources.mandala.library.virginia.edu/node/109566)). The implication for an RCT is uncomfortable. Untrained respondents under-report drift because they do not notice it. Training raises noticing. A reverse-keyed item such as "I often find myself on autopilot" could therefore get *worse* in the treatment arm while the construct improves. The facet's items should be positively keyed and framed as catching or noticing a drift, and this facet should get no reversal.

Two more domain cases set limits on what reversals should say. When the MAIA was revised, its only reverse-scored subscales, Not-Distracting and Not-Worrying, were the persistently unreliable ones. The fix added items without changing their direction and reached only **α = .74 and .67**. The authors accepted reverse scoring as "a minor evil" and reported lower alphas in samples without mind–body experience ([Mehling et al. 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0208034)). That is a reliability × experience interaction, which in an RCT could show up as differential reliability between arms at post-test. The Self-Compassion Scale's negative items describe a different behaviour rather than a negated one, and they still formed a quasi-separate factor. Its supporters locate **95% of reliable variance in a general factor** across 20 samples ([Neff et al. 2019](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Neff.inpress.pdf)). Its critics show meta-analytically that the negative half relates far more strongly to psychopathology than the positive half ([Muris & Otgaar, PMC9095813](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9095813/)). For Sense Foraging, **reversals in Normalising stress, View under stress and Safety must not be the only distress-laden items in their facets**. If they are, keying becomes confounded with negative affect.

This evidence cuts both ways, and the report's central point lies here. The Van Dam DIF is usually cited as a cost of reversed items. Read from the other side, it says something more important: **practice raises agreement with positively worded items beyond what the trait warrants**. On a mixed scale, that shows up as divergence between positively and reverse-keyed items, which can be detected and modelled. On an all-positive scale, the same effect is invisible and counts as treatment benefit. The domain literature's resolutions so far have been to model a bifactor, add items, avoid reversals altogether, or score the halves separately. None shows that reversals *improve* measurement in contemplative samples, and the notes found no RCT that tested invariance of a wording factor across arms. What the domain does show is that reversals are where training effects on *reading* become visible.

## An all-positive outcome cannot tell learning the language from improving

The case for reversals rests on identification, and here the literature is unanimous. "All methods designed to correct acquiescence bias rely on contradiction in survey responses." In a fully unbalanced scale, "this contradiction does not exist, rendering acquiescence impossible to identify" ([Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf)). Every model of acquiescent responding "requires both regular and reversed items", and the separation gets harder the fewer reversed items there are ([Plieninger & Heck 2018](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/49190/1/Plieninger-Heck_2018_A-new-model-for-Acquiescence.pdf)). An all-positive scale also hides the problem in exploratory analysis: in unbalanced scales, not extracting an acquiescence factor did not affect loading recovery ([D'Urso et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC10177316&resultType=core&format=json)). Acquiescence merges into the content factors. **A clean, high-loading all-positive solution is what contamination looks like, not evidence that there is none.**

Acquiescence is small per item and large in aggregate. It accounts for about **3–5% of item variance** and inflates same-direction inter-item correlations and therefore reliability ([Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf)). About 6% of respondents answer in a pronounced acquiescent or disacquiescent way ([Hinz et al. 2007](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2736523/)). On 34 positively keyed items, that shared variance adds up coherently in the sum score. In simulations, unbalanced and uncorrected scales "inevitably" attenuated validity coefficients. Attenuation was worst **below 10 items and with low loadings**, which describes Sense Foraging's 2–5-item facets exactly ([Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf)). The trait is also stable within a person: test–retest is about .6 at two months and at four years ([de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf)), and 49–59% of acquiescence variance is trait-like over eight years ([Wetzel et al. 2016](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/73876)).

That stability makes the RCT argument precise rather than rhetorical. The stable part of each person's acquiescence cancels in a pre–post comparison, and randomization balances its level across arms. So **acquiescence alone does not bias the treatment effect**. What does bias it is a change in agreement behaviour that differs by arm. A simulation found response-style bias "may be substantial if the attribute of interest is correlated with response style" and "generally very small" otherwise ([Plieninger 2017](https://metatoc.com/papers/53500-mountain-or-molehill-a-simulation-study-on-the-impact-of-response-styles)). In an RCT, the "attribute" is treatment assignment. A course that teaches "sensing mode" and "doing mode" gives treated participants familiar phrases to agree with, a reason to please the people teaching them, and practice-linked DIF of the kind Van Dam documented. Any of these correlates agreement with arm, which is the high-bias case. Unbalanced scales can produce "spurious… inflated… or hidden" associations ([Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf)). On an all-positive outcome, such a shift is arithmetically identical to improvement. With reversals, the two leave different fingerprints. True improvement raises agreement with positive items and lowers it with reversed ones. An agreement shift raises both. **No study has measured acquiescence-induced bias in an RCT effect or tested whether an intervention changes acquiescence.** The notes looked and found only stability and response-shift work. The threat is inferred from mechanism, not observed. It is still the one threat a reviewer of a course-language outcome will raise first, and only opposite-keyed items can answer it.

The alternatives to in-scale reversals are weaker for this purpose. Contradiction can be imported from a separate balanced block of unrelated items. Swatton identified a person-intercept acquiescence factor on an unbalanced scale this way. But that "necessarily strengthens the assumption", because respondents must now acquiesce equally on every scale in the model ([Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf)). Cross-domain consistency of acquiescence is moderate to high ([Danner et al. 2015](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/54554/)), but items are not affected equally ([Kam & Zhou 2015](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC5965517&resultType=core&format=json)). The Ferrando group finds "no convincing evidence" that dedicated acquiescence scales help ([Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf)). An external block also cannot see agreement that is specific to the course vocabulary, which is the RCT's distinctive threat. Instructed-response checks catch gross inattention but say nothing about acquiescence, and they produce false positives even among thoughtful respondents ([Curran & Hauser 2019](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/2019-curran.pdf)). Item-specific response formats outperform agree–disagree on quality ([Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)), but adopting them would mean rewriting the instrument and abandoning the settled format. They belong on the list for a later version, not in pool 6.

## Pool 6: fourteen contradictory reversals, spread thin and paired

How many is the question the evidence answers least well. The random-intercept model in theory needs only one opposite-keyed item, but "how many additional indicators are required in practice will need to be discovered" ([Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf)). The only partial-balance simulation used **34% reversed items** in every condition. There, random-intercept factor analysis recovered the structure with congruence above .95 in 83% of 486 conditions and beat the EFA-with-target alternative in 79% of them. The authors warned that greater imbalance "might have more detrimental effects" and did not test it ([de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf)). Eight to ten reversals among 34 items, about 20%, would sit outside the tested range and inside the "few among many" pattern that Weijters et al. warn against. Full balance would mean ~68 items on a phone, with all the costs of mixing at their maximum. So the recommendation aims for the tested ratio, at a length phones can carry.

The build notes list 32 scored items and two checks in 34 slots. The figures below use the brief's 34; the difference changes none of the conclusions. Adding 14 reversals gives ~48 items, **29% reversed**. As arithmetic, assuming equal acquiescence loadings, the keyed mean then carries (34 − 14)/48 ≈ 0.42 of the acquiescence that an all-positive mean carries. Real loadings are unequal, and reversed items tend to load *more* on acquiescence ([de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf)), which helps cancellation. Spread them as **one per facet in every facet except Drift awareness (10), plus a second in the four largest clusters** (Action, View core, View openness, and whichever of Completeness or Labelling is larger). Spreading avoids the FFMQ trap of a facet that is all reversed, keeps acquiescence from being confounded with any one facet's content, and lets a wording factor be separated from facet factors. Draft about two candidates per slot (~28), cognitive-interview them on phones, and keep the 14 whose meaning survives. Adding the new items changes the REB-approved item set, so pool 6 goes into the ethics amendment.

| Recommendation | Evidence grade | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Include opposite-keyed items at all (for acquiescence identification and the RCT) | **Strong** for identification; **inference** for the size of the RCT threat | Swatton 2024; Plieninger & Heck 2018; Weijters et al. 2013; Plieninger 2017 |
| ~14 reversals, ~30% of the pool | **Moderate**: matches the only tested ratio; lower ratios untested | de la Fuente & Abad 2020 |
| Spread across facets, never a whole facet reversed | **Moderate** | FFMQ/PHLMS confounds; Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando balanced-core logic (via de la Fuente & Abad 2020) |
| No reversal in Drift awareness | **Moderate** (domain-specific) | Van Dam et al. 2010; Levin-Aspenson et al. 2023 |
| Antonym or opposite-stance wording, no negations | **Moderate**; antonym evidence conflicts | Ilhan et al. 2024; Dykema et al. 2022; Baumgartner et al. 2018 slides |
| Contradictory, not contrary, opposites | **Weak–moderate** (theory plus indirect data) | Elek et al. 2025; Kam et al. 2021; WCS 2010 "disagree both" pattern |
| Disperse, and separate each reversal from its partner | **Moderate**; one null replication (title only) | Weijters et al. 2009; WBS 2013 |
| Balanced external marker block | **Weak**; optional, RCT only | Swatton 2024; Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025 |

### Ten drafting rules for the reversed items

Antonyms versus negations is the one place where the evidence genuinely conflicts. The only study that estimated the two separately found the **largest misresponse for polar-opposite items** (loading shortfall −.55, 95% CI [−.97, −.15], against −.17 for negation, whose interval spans zero) ([Baumgartner 2018 slides on Baumgartner, Weijters & Pieters 2018](https://wiwi.univie.ac.at/fileadmin/user_upload/f_wiwi/News/Events/2018/Baumgartner.pdf)). These are slide figures from one illustrative item set. Zhang et al.'s all-reversed version that mixed polar opposites with negations also needed a method factor on its polar-opposite items ([Zhang et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4909292/)). Yet Ilhan et al. found antonyms clearly better than negations. The reconciliation is that the polar opposites that fail are *non-canonical contraries*: "quiet" is not the logical negation of "talkative", so a middling person can agree with both ([Elek et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full); [Steinmann 2024](https://www.iea.nl/sites/default/files/2024-04/To-Mix-or-Not-to-Mix-Positively-and-Negatively-Worded-Items.pdf)). The rules below therefore ask for opposites that are canonical and contradictory, not merely antonyms.

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reverse the construct, not the sentence.** Describe the presence of the facet's low-pole stance or behaviour, not the absence of the skill. | Absence-of-deficit scoring failed in the MAAS ([Van Dam et al. 2010](https://sources.mandala.library.virginia.edu/node/109566)); a reversal can drift into a neighbouring construct, as with "I have all the things I need" in the Material Values Scale ([Baumgartner slides](https://wiwi.univie.ac.at/fileadmin/user_upload/f_wiwi/News/Events/2018/Baumgartner.pdf)) |
| 2 | **No "not", "never", "don't", "un-", "non-", "-less"** in a stem, and never a reversal of a facet already named as a negation ("non-judgement"). | Disagreeing with a negation is a double negative ([Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)); negations cost ω and shifted means ([Ilhan et al. 2024](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440235.pdf)); a short "not" is easy to miss on a phone (inference) |
| 3 | **Apply the middle-person test**: could someone moderate on this facet sensibly *disagree* with both the reversal and its partner? If yes, rewrite. | Contraries make mid-trait inconsistency logical ([Elek et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)); in the no-midpoint format, misresponse is mostly "disagree with both" ([WCS 2010](https://public.vlerick.com/Publications/74eb09c8-6aa9-e011-8a89-005056a635ed.pdf)) |
| 4 | **Pair each reversal with one named positive item in the same facet**, matched on intensity, length, reading level, time frame and referent. | Reversals cancel bias only if answered "as extremely as their counterparts" ([Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)); matched severity produced the best antonym form ([Ilhan et al. 2024](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440235.pdf)); pairs enable screening |
| 5 | **Use the same course vocabulary as the partner item** ("sensing mode", "doing mode"), so that familiarity with the vocabulary is balanced across keying. | Otherwise keying is confounded with jargon that only the treatment arm learns (inference). The pre-item definition equalizes vocabulary only partly |
| 6 | **No reversal whose truth depends on insight the course trains** (lapse frequency, autopilot, mind-wandering counts). | Reported lapses rise as noticing improves ([Van Dam et al. 2010](https://sources.mandala.library.virginia.edu/node/109566)); the autopilot facet failed scalar invariance over treatment ([Levin-Aspenson et al. 2023](https://contextualscience.org/publications/levinaspenson_marks_dalrymple_zimmerman_2023)) |
| 7 | **Describe a stance, not a symptom**, and never make the reversal the only aversive-content item in its facet. | Negative SCS and MAIA items track distress and psychopathology ([PMC9095813](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9095813/); [Mehling et al. 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0208034)) |
| 8 | **Use "hard / difficult" only where ability is the construct** (the Action/self-efficacy facet); elsewhere use frequency or stance opposites. | A difficulty framing imports ability, effort and distress (inference from canonicity principles in the item-writing notes) |
| 9 | **One clause, short, no double-barrelling.** | [Clark & Watson 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30896212); online and less attentive samples generate method factors ([Takahashi et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.833381/full)); low reading ability drives wording variance ([Gnambs & Schroeders](https://kobra.uni-kassel.de/handle/123456789/12439)) |
| 10 | **Over-draft two to one, then cognitive-interview on phones** and probe specifically for misread reversals. | Pre-testing catches poor wording ([Boateng et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2018.00149)); Baumgartner's eye tracking shows "difficulty" misresponse despite longer reading |

Rules 1, 3 and 8 are easiest to see side by side. The rows below are patterns for the lab to adapt, not proposed items. For Completeness/non-judgement, "I don't judge my sensations" breaks rule 2. The presence-of-opposite form is "I sort sensations into good and bad as soon as I notice them". For Openness to ambiguity, "I am not comfortable with unclear sensations" breaks rule 2. "When a sensation is unclear, I need to pin down what it is straight away" states the opposite stance. For Action, "I find it hard to shift into the sensing mode when I choose to" is acceptable, because efficacy *is* ability. For Drift, the right move is no reversal at all. "I often find myself in the doing mode without realising it" breaks rule 6 however it is phrased.

### Placement inside the constrained shuffle

Study 1 already uses a seeded, constrained interleave (pages of 8–9, facets kept apart, checks never first or last on a page). The reversal rules can be added to the same search. Correlations between reversed pairs grow stronger as the distance between the two items increases, because nearby reversals push respondents to "retrieve new and different information" ([Weijters, Geuens & Schillewaert 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ijrema/v26y2009i1p2-12.html)). A later paper titled "Proximity of items does not necessarily affect their correlation" suggests the effect does not always replicate. Only its title was seen ([Hogrefe](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1015-5759/a000661)). The constraints to add are these. Put **each reversal on a different page from its partner**. Never place two reversals adjacently. Avoid runs of more than about five positive items, so that no reversal arrives after a long same-direction run ([WBS 2013](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/4099670/6801377)). Spread about two or three reversals per page. Never put a reversal in the opening position or immediately beside an attention check, so that the two screens are not confounded. Keep the response options in the same left-to-right order on every item, so that reversal happens in content only. Record positions, as the platform already does, so that proximity can be tested in the data.

## Analysis plan: model the method, flag the inconsistent, test invariance

The analysis must be pre-registered before Study 1. Its job is to stop a wording factor from being named as a facet, and to stop a "corrected" or "uncorrected" result from being chosen after the fact.

**Screening comes first, and inconsistency is a flag, not an exclusion rule.** The lab's rule, exclude on failing both instructed-response checks and flag on failing one, already respects Curran and Hauser's finding that each such item catches some thoughtful respondents ([Curran & Hauser 2019](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/2019-curran.pdf)). Add per-page response time and longstring. Add a within-person antonym index computed over the pre-specified reversal pairs. Following Curran's convention, a pair is used only if its sample correlation is −.60 or lower ([careless package](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/packages/careless/index.html)). Report each respondent's count of same-side pairs. Do not exclude on these indices in the primary analysis. Inconsistency is predicted by reading comprehension and by mid-trait position, as well as by carelessness ([Steinmann 2024](https://www.iea.nl/sites/default/files/2024-04/To-Mix-or-Not-to-Mix-Positively-and-Negatively-Worded-Items.pdf); [Elek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12581211/)). Excluding on it would skew the sample toward high-verbal respondents. Instead, re-run the factor solution without flagged respondents as a sensitivity analysis. If the wording factor collapses, as in [Arias et al. 2020](https://produccioncientifica.usal.es/documentos/60055ebb68c34960007088fc), it was carelessness. Tabulate "Prefer not to answer" rates by keying, and compare inconsistency by first language and by recruitment source (general versus contemplative-leaning posting, Prolific versus students).

| Stage | Primary model | Pre-specified checks and sensitivity analyses |
|---|---|---|
| **Study 1 EFA** (N ≈ 450–500, SFS only) | Polychoric correlations, WLSMV, items **unrecoded**. Random-intercept ESEM with target rotation to the hypothesized facets ([de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf); [Aichholzer 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4251787/)) | Screen the flagged near-duplicate pairs (4–13, 16–18, 1–9, 6–16) for residual correlations *before* estimating acquiescence, because an acquiescence factor absorbs doublets ([Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf)). Parallel analysis with and without the random intercept. **A factor defined mainly by reversals is a method factor, not a facet.** Sensitivity analyses: the Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando / `siren` estimate ([CRAN](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/siren/refman/siren.html)); a solution on positive items only; a solution without flagged respondents |
| **Item retention** | Keep a reversal if, in the random-intercept solution, it loads on its intended facet comparably to that facet's positive items and correlates clearly negatively with its partner | Report each item's misresponse rate. Retention thresholds are the lab's to pre-register; the notes supply none beyond Curran's −.60 for screening pairs |
| **Study 2 CFA** (N ≈ 450–500, plus FFMQ-15, MAIA-2, BIDR-16) | Correlated-facets CFA plus a random-intercept acquiescence factor (all loadings fixed to 1 on unrecoded items, orthogonal to content); test its variance > 0 ([Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman 2006](https://www.academia.edu/475094/Random_intercept_item_factor_analysis); [Savalei & Falk 2014](https://www.psych.mcgill.ca/perpg/fac/falk/publication/savalei-recovering-2014-1)) | Compare with an added reversed-item method factor, to separate acquiescence from residual wording variance. | **RCT** (4-week course) | Final-scale keyed score in a baseline-adjusted ANCOVA (standard trial practice, not from the notes) | Longitudinal random-intercept CFA: test the acquiescence factor's mean and variance by arm × time. Configural → metric → scalar invariance across time within each arm and across arms at post, with the method factor modelled. Treatment effect estimated **separately on positive and reversed items**. Positive-items-only sensitivity analysis. Re-run without flagged respondents |

Random-intercept factor analysis is the default because three independent simulation programmes rank it best on accuracy for its simplicity. It adds one parameter and is "robust to the violation of its assumptions" ([Savalei & Falk 2014](https://www.psych.mcgill.ca/perpg/fac/falk/publication/savalei-recovering-2014-1); [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf)). Its known weakness is acquiescence loadings that vary strongly across items. That is why the target-rotation estimate is kept as a sensitivity check, not discarded. Ordinal estimation is also a substantive choice, not just a technical one. A 2025 review reports that the wording-factor problem "is significantly reduced" when a non-linear factor model replaces a linear one ([Elek et al. 2025, citing Rečka et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)). The simulations covered N = 200–1,000, but the notes do not report how sample size affected recovery. With ~48 items and up to 11 target factors, Study 1 should stay at the top of its planned range. The 6-point format adds an interpretive caution. Because its inconsistency is mostly "disagree with both", the random intercept will absorb forced ambivalence as well as yea-saying. Its variance therefore measures a general agreement style, not acquiescence in the narrow sense.

The RCT's split analysis reads as a diagnostic grid. It can rule out the worst interpretation even where it cannot settle the subtler ones.

| Treatment-arm change, raw agreement | Positive items | Reversed items | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content change | rises | falls | Construct improved; scales agree |
| Acquiescence or "course echo" | rises | rises | Agreement shifted; positive-only effect is inflated |
| Training-specific DIF / response shift | rises | flat | Van Dam pattern: positive items over-credit practice; examine invariance |
| Insight effect | flat or rises | rises on lapse-type content | Should not occur if rule 6 is followed; if it does, a reversal is reading insight |

Two expectations keep this grid honest. First, some non-invariance of the method factor is *expected* and is not proof of bias. Mid-trait respondents generate the most logical inconsistency, so a course that moves people off the middle should shrink the wording variance in the treatment arm, even with no change in how items are read. That is an inference from the Kam et al. pattern, and the lab could be the first to test it. Second, a 4-week course is short, but the FFMQ's configural structure changed across 8 weeks of MBCT ([Gu et al. 2016](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f55f8f8e-e686-41d1-89dd-d4651490c277)). Labelling, Completeness and Drift content, which the course explicitly teaches people to re-perceive, is where partial invariance is most likely needed.

The plan needs one decision rule written down in advance: **what happens if the reversals fail in Study 1**. Failure would look like weak partner correlations, high misresponse, or a wording factor that survives both the random intercept and the removal of flagged respondents. In that case, score the scale from its positive items, but **keep the best 4–6 reversal pairs in the RCT as administered, unscored acquiescence probes**. They still supply the contradiction that identifies the random intercept and the positive/reversed split, without their wording problems entering the outcome score. The psychometric-costs notes propose this "administer but don't score" design as the defensible fallback. It means the RCT keeps its alibi whatever Study 1 shows. An optional 6–8-item balanced block of unrelated content would add one more distinction in the RCT: whether the course shifted agreement in general (the block moves) or only on course vocabulary (it does not). The evidence for such blocks is weak, so it is worth including only if session length allows.

## Conclusion

The debate over reversed items is usually framed as a trade: worse factor structure in exchange for acquiescence control. For this instrument the trade looks different. The costs fall mainly on the *structure* of an exploratory analysis, where a pre-registered method model can contain them. The benefit falls on the *validity of a causal claim*, where nothing done afterwards can recover it. That is the asymmetry the report turns on. A wording factor in Study 1 is an inconvenience. An RCT effect on an all-positive scale that echoes the course's own vocabulary is an effect no reviewer can be shown is not agreement. The no-midpoint format raises the stakes of drafting quality more than of quantity. Its misresponse is mostly mid-trait respondents disagreeing with both items of a loose pair, so the middle-person test in rule 3 probably matters more than the exact count of 14.

The lab is also in a position to produce evidence the literature lacks. No published study reports how an intervention moves positively and reverse-keyed items separately, how a wording factor behaves across arms, or whether a random-intercept model holds up at about 30% reversed items in a 6-point format. Pre-registering the split analysis, the invariance tests and the fallback would make pool 6 a methodological contribution as well as a better outcome measure. The results would tell the next contemplative-measurement team whether practice changes what people experience or only what they agree with.
