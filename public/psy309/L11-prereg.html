2 · The worked example’s version

Both studies’ data were simulated in R. In Study 2, 256 participants per condition were randomly assigned to gratitude or neutral journaling; this n is four times the 64 per condition needed to detect d = 0.5 with 80% power, because the primary question is an interaction. Stress was drawn from a normal distribution (M = 20, SD = 4), and optimism was generated as 50 − 0.78 × (Stress − 20) + 5 × Gratitude + 0.60 × (Stress − 20) × Gratitude + noise (SD = 9.5), with seed 4026. The primary outcome is optimism. No participants were excluded. The primary analysis is a 2 (condition) × 2 (stress: median split) ANOVA, with the condition effect within each stress group as Bonferroni-corrected simple effects. Any other analysis is reported as exploratory.

From the Practical 5 worked example (Tier 2). Your version uses your own values, model, seed and tier.

3 · Yours, in this order

  1. Say plainly that the data were simulated in R.
  2. For each study: the n and the power analysis behind it.
  3. The values and model you simulated from, and the seed.
  4. The primary DV, and who is in the analysis.
  5. The one primary analysis per study. Then one sentence: everything else is exploratory.

For a real study, add two more

  • An exclusion rule written before data collection: for the gratitude study, “anyone who completes fewer than five of the seven journaling evenings is excluded”.
  • A stopping rule: data collection ends at the planned n, whatever the results look like on the way.

Your simulated data can’t miss an evening, so your paper doesn’t need these. Your Discussion can still say what you’d preregister if you ran it for real.
