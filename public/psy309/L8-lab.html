Your Study 2 as a 2 × 2

Practical 4 not finished? It’s due this Friday, Nov 6. Today’s lab block is for that: sit with a TA. Come back to this page during reading for Practical 5.

Practical 4 done or nearly? Then this is the Practical 5 dry run. You will write your hypothesis as six numbers, simulate the experiment, and see the 2 × 2 it predicts.

1 · Your six numbers

NumberWhere it comes from
n_perYour Tier 1 n per condition, times 4. An interaction is a difference between differences and needs about four times the sample.
cov_m, cov_sdYour covariate’s mean and SD, from your Practical 4 measure.
slopeYour Practical 4 r × SD(DV) ÷ SD(covariate). The worked example: −.31 × 10 ÷ 4 = −0.78.
effectWhat the manipulation adds at an average covariate score, in DV points. From your Practical 5 papers.
moderHow much that effect grows (+) or shrinks (−) per covariate point. Your theory picks the sign; 0 means no moderation.

The DV is simulated on a 0–100 scale with mean 50 and SD 10. If yours isn’t, convert effect to that scale, or change the 50 and the 10 in part 2.

2 · Copy, paste into RStudio, run

library(tidyverse)

# 1. Your numbers. Change these six lines, nothing else.
n_per  <- 256    # per condition: your Tier 1 n, times 4
cov_m  <- 20     # your covariate's mean ...
cov_sd <- 4      # ... and SD (from your Practical 4 measure)
slope  <- -0.78  # covariate -> DV: your r x SD(DV) / SD(covariate)
effect <- 5      # what the manipulation adds, at average covariate
moder  <- 0.60   # how much that effect changes per covariate point

# 2. Simulate the experiment from that hypothesis
set.seed(309)
d <- tibble(
  Condition = factor(rep(c("Control", "Treatment"), each = n_per),
                     levels = c("Control", "Treatment")),
  Cov = rnorm(2 * n_per, cov_m, cov_sd)
) %>%
  mutate(Tx = as.numeric(Condition == "Treatment"),
         DV = 50 + slope * (Cov - cov_m) + effect * Tx +
              moder * (Cov - cov_m) * Tx + rnorm(n(), 0, 10))

# 3. Median split, then the 2 x 2
d$Cov_Cat <- cut(d$Cov, breaks = c(-Inf, median(d$Cov), Inf),
                 labels = c("Lower", "Higher"))
summary(aov(DV ~ Condition * Cov_Cat, data = d))

# 4. The four cell means, and the picture
cells <- d %>% group_by(Cov_Cat, Condition) %>%
  summarise(M = mean(DV), SE = sd(DV) / sqrt(n()), .groups = "drop")
cells
ggplot(cells, aes(Condition, M, fill = Condition)) +
  geom_col(alpha = .6, width = .6) +
  geom_errorbar(aes(ymin = M - SE, ymax = M + SE), width = .2) +
  facet_wrap(~ Cov_Cat) + theme_classic() + theme(legend.position = "none")

With the numbers as given (the gratitude worked example), the treatment adds under 1 point in the lower half and about 6 in the higher half, and the interaction is significant.

3 · Read it like a grid

  1. From cells: the treatment’s effect in the Lower half, then in the Higher half. Subtract.
  2. Different? That’s your interaction. Say it in simple effects: “In the higher-___ half, ___ raised ___ by __ points; in the lower half, by only __.”
  3. Now set moder <- 0 and run again. That is what “no moderation” looks like with your numbers.
