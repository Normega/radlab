Part one

Refusing to choose

The design of nearly every clinical trial

Take the within-versus-between trade-off — and refuse to choose.

A mixed design has at least one between-subjects factor and at least one within-subjects factor.

People randomized to groups (between). Everyone measured over time (within). Understand one mixed design deeply and you can read most of the intervention literature.

Time × Group

Randomize to a mindfulness programme or a waitlist (between). Measure wellbeing before and after (within). Now run the factorial checklist — and watch what each effect means:

  • Main effect of Time — everyone changed. Uninteresting alone: maturation, history and regression all live here.
  • Main effect of Group — the groups differ on average. Also not the story.
  • The Time × Group interaction — did the groups change differently?

That cell is the finding. A clinical trial's entire causal claim lives in one interaction.

How it's written

Wellbeing ~ Time * Group + (1|Participant)

Fixed effects — Time, Group, their interaction. The questions you're asking.

Random effect — (1|Participant). The design's within-ness, formally acknowledged.

That last term gets its own section after the break. First: a dataset that should disturb you.

Reading the graph

Two lines over time.

  • Parallel — both groups drift alike. No treatment effect, whatever the slopes.
  • Converging or diverging — the interaction. The finding.

Once you have this template, intervention studies read at a glance — and so do their abuses. A single improving line with no comparison group is the one-group pretest/posttest again, with better production values.

Exercise A · 25 minutes · cloud R

Build Simpson's Paradox

Studying more will make grades worse — while, inside every single department, studying more makes them better.

Both statements, true of the same data.

Open L8_simpsons.R.

Check-in position 2. Hold the pooled scatterplot on screen, with its confident downward line, BEFORE the colours go on. Ask what a news story would say. Then colour by department.

Same data, opposite sign

Correlation of hours studied with grades
All students pooled−0.67
Department A+0.68
Department B+0.81
Department C+0.63

The trick is that departments differ in two ways at once: some students study more, and those same departments grade harder.

Three positive numbers, one negative number, one dataset. Which one is "the" answer?

Neither number is "the" answer

A student asks: "Should I study more?"

A within-department question. The answer is yes — emphatically.

A dean asks: "If students across the university studied more, would average grades rise?"

A between-department question, tangled up with who studies where.

The paradox isn't that the numbers disagree. It's that we casually assume one number could answer both questions.

The repair is structural: model the grouping. (1|Department) is, quite literally, Simpson insurance.

The person-level version matters for psychology's soul: a variable's relationship ACROSS people and WITHIN a person are different facts. Most theories are about the within-person one; most data are between-person.

Part two

Everyone starts somewhere different

The picture that explains everything

Plot each participant's scores across the study as their own thin line. Forty lines.

  • The lines start at wildly different heights. Some people are simply happier, calmer, faster, more anxious — before anyone manipulates anything.
  • The lines slope more similarly than they start. Your manipulation's push is usually modest compared to the standing differences between people.

Think of something where people's baselines vary more than any experiment could move them. Almost everything you care about qualifies.

Fixed and random, in one sentence each

Fixed effects are the questions you're asking — the average slope, the group difference, the interaction. Assumed to be the same process for everyone; estimated once.

Random effects acknowledge that each participant carries their own baseline. (1|Participant) says: give every person their own intercept, and don't charge those differences against the treatment's account.

That's the examinable core. No lmer() on the test.

What acknowledging baselines buys

  • Power. Baseline differences are usually the biggest source of variance; giving them their own term removes them from the noise your effect must shout over. Same magic as the within-subjects design — expressed as a model instead of a procedure.
  • Honesty about repeated measures. Two measurements from the same person are not two independent people. Treating them as such counts evidence twice and shrinks p-values fraudulently.
  • Simpson insurance. Modelling within-person effects separately from between-person differences is exactly the disaggregation you just watched.

A paired t-test is secretly a tiny mixed model. So is every "each person is their own control" argument you've met.

Why this is now psychology's default

Once you see (1|Participant), you see the pattern everywhere: students in classrooms, trials in subjects, patients in clinics, measurements in days.

Real data comes in clusters whose members resemble each other. The model that admits it wins.

You won't fit these this term. But when your thesis dataset arrives with repeated measures — and it will — you'll recognize the shape and know why the analysis looks like that.

Exercise B · 25 minutes · Test 2 prep

Sort the third variable

Ten scenarios. Mediator, moderator, or confound?

The mnemonic:
Moderation = when / for whom
Mediation = why
Confound = controlling for

The test that decides it — the arrows:

Mediator: receives an arrow from X.
Confound: sends arrows to both X and Y, receives none.
Moderator: receives nothing; changes the size of X→Y across its own levels.

Vote first, justify second. Five rounds of two.

Check-in position 3 — this quiz DOES have an answer key; reveal after each round. Budget the last eight minutes for items 9 and 10.

The two that matter

Item 9 — the expensive mistake

Poverty predicts poorer health. A researcher notes poverty produces chronic stress, controls for chronic stress, finds the poverty–health link shrinks sharply, and concludes poverty matters less than believed.

Chronic stress is a mediator being treated as a confound.

Controlling it away doesn't purify the estimate — it amputates the explanation and then reports the effect as small. With real policy consequences.

What should they have run instead? A mediation analysis: how much of poverty's effect travels through stress.

The two that matter

Item 10 — the honest complication

Students who attend office hours earn better grades. Conscientiousness predicts attending office hours — and studying more, submitting on time, asking for help.

Confound. But push on it: if conscientious students benefit more from office hours than others do, it's also a moderator.

A variable's role is a claim about causal structure, not a property of the variable.

The same variable can play different roles in different questions. Which is why the arrows are the whole game.

A worked short answer

Earn the marks, again

“A wellness office invites students to sign up for a therapy-dog program. Sign-ups complete a stress measure before the first session and after the fourth weekly session, which falls just after midterms end. Stress drops significantly (p = .01). The office concludes the program reduces stress and requests funding to expand it.” Evaluate.

  • Design: one-group pretest/posttest — no comparison condition. (Name it.)
  • Selection: volunteers who sign up for a stress program differ from students at large — and likely enrolled when stress peaked, so regression to the mean is live.
  • History: the post-test lands right after midterms end. Stress falls then for everyone.
  • Statistics: p = .01 with no effect size, no interval, no n — how big, and how sure?
  • The repair: randomize sign-ups to dogs-now vs a waitlist; the Time × Group interaction is the finding.

Five marks, five named concepts. The dogs may well work — this study cannot know.

Reveal point by point, room proposes first. Note the repair uses last hour's mixed-design language — say so out loud.
