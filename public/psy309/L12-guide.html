1 · What the numbers mean

Score each poster out of 10 on both criteria. These are the anchors the class agreed on.

ScoreEngagingInformative
9–10I’d stop at this poster at a conference and stay for the whole thingI could explain their design, result and its limits to someone else afterwards
7–8Held my attention; clear hook, clean figureI understood the study; one or two things I’d ask about
5–6I followed it, but drifted; dense or flat in placesThe claim is there but the design or numbers are hazy
3–4Hard to stay with; text-heavy or disorganizedI can’t reconstruct what they did
1–2I stopped paying attentionI don’t know what was studied

Judge the communication, not the result. A modest or null-shaped finding presented honestly can outscore an oversold one.

Use the range. Five posters genuinely differ. Scores far from the group average are checked.

Before you submit each review

  • Your strength names something. “The hook image made the question land before you spoke”, not “great job”.
  • Your improvement is something the author could act on. “The axis labels were too small to read”, not “figure unclear”.
  • You criticized the poster, not the study. Not “their sample was small”, but “did the poster say what its sample can’t tell us?”
