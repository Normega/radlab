1. The action editor's role

You do not need to be the topic expert

You may be assigned a paper outside your own specialty. That is normal. Your job is not to supply all the content or methods expertise yourself; it is to find a few capable people who can, through peer review, or to desk reject a submission that has serious flaws and should not go out.

Reviews inform your decision; they do not make it

Peer reviews are there to help you decide, and you may disagree with them. It can be justified to reject a paper with two positive reviews, or to accept one with negative reviews, when your own reading of the evidence supports it. Explain your reasoning in the decision letter.

If you are unsure about a decision at any point, ask your senior editor for a second opinion. That is what they are there for.

2. Finding reviewers

This is often the hardest part of the job. Everyone is overloaded with review requests, and journals without long-established name recognition have a harder time getting acceptances. These habits help.

Where to look

  • Start with the journal's reviewer database. People who have reviewed for the journal before, and who have a history of accepting requests, are your best first candidates.
  • Look one step beyond the reference list. If the obvious names are unavailable, consider co-authors of the work the manuscript cites. A PhD student or recent graduate is often fully up to date on the topic and receives far fewer requests than senior authors.

How to ask

  • Personalize the invitation. Manuscript systems provide boilerplate emails that read as impersonal. Rewrite the opening in a conversational voice and say why you are asking this person in particular.
  • Mention it when their work is cited. For example: "The authors build on arguments from your recent work, so I would be grateful for your expert view of their study."

How many, and how often

  • Invite several people at once. Start with 4 to 6 invitations rather than one at a time.
  • Expect to invite 10 to 15 people to secure 2 reviews. Plan for this from the start; it is not a sign that something has gone wrong.
  • Check the manuscript system every few days until both reviewers are confirmed, and send new invitations as declines come in.
  • If you are stuck, ask for help. Your senior editor can suggest names or step in.
