You're going on a walk. The path will fork eight times, and where it leads depends on what your senses notice.

At each fork, notice what you see, hear and feel right now, and let that choose the way. There are no right answers. Notice what each side offers, and go the way you lean.

If nothing comes, take the faint path in the middle: nothing right now. That counts too.

Each part of this place came from something you noticed on the way. Another day you will notice other things, and the path will go somewhere else.

On this walk you were sense foraging: shifting your attention from doing to sensing, taking in what is here through your senses, with curiosity.

Why this?

Segal and Farb (2026) report that in remitted depression, the patients whose sensory brain regions were most inhibited during sadness had an eightfold increase in the risk of recurrence over two years of follow-up. They propose sense foraging as a way to keep sensation available as a resource in the long-term management of mood disorders.
