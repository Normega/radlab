The mental model (read once — everything else follows)

A student claims a gap (a declared missing piece of a page), reads a primary source, writes a short summary, and submits it with the source attached. On submission, the system reads the actual source and compares it with what the student wrote. You then make the one judgement the machine can't be trusted with alone: does the student's text say what their cited source says?

  • Accept → the system drafts the page section from the source (the student's summary is evidence of their reading, not the text that gets published) and files the draft in Review as a pending proposal. The student's name goes in the page history.
  • Send back → the claim returns to the student with your note. They revise and resubmit. A send-back is feedback, not a penalty — let your note's tone say so.

Nothing you do publishes anything by itself. Publication is a separate, deliberate step in the Review queue. No path — for you, for students, or for the AI — writes into the textbook without a human pressing Publish.

Working the Submissions queue

Your section. The queue opens on your third of the class, split by student surname — A–G Nidal · H–N Maryam · O–Z Whitney — with the counts shown on the tab. The Everyone tab is one tap away and exists for covering a colleague; nothing is hidden from any of you. Submissions the system can't classify by surname appear in everyone's section on purpose, so no submission can fall between the cracks. If your loads drift badly uneven mid-term, tell Norm — rebalancing is a one-line change.

Each card shows the student's summary, their cited source (open it — the link is right there), and a coloured verdict badge from the automatic comparison:

BadgeMeaningYour move
summary matchesSource supports the summarySkim, spot-check one claim against the source, usually Accept
minor issuesRight reading, small slipsRead the comparison note; Accept if the slips don't distort, else send back naming them
summary divergesReal contradictions foundRead the note and the source. If you agree, Send back — the note is pre-seeded into your message; edit it into your own words
unclearThe comparison couldn't tellGenuinely yours — read the source
awaiting source checkComparison hasn't runPress the compare button before deciding
verdict not recordedNo stored verdictTreat as unclear — never assume agreement from silence

The badge is evidence, not a decision. It has been wrong in both directions during testing — including once calling a divergent summary a match. Read the comparison commentary shown beside the student's text, then check the source yourself for anything surprising. You are the reviewer; the model is a fast first-reader.

“Full read” means the system really read the full text, not just an abstract. A verdict over an abstract (or a conference-abstract DOI) is worth much less — weight accordingly.

Send-back notes are current-state, not a chat thread: the student sees exactly one note — the last one written. Make it complete on its own: what's wrong, what to fix, resubmit. For divergent verdicts the comparison note is pre-filled as your draft; keep the substance, humanize the tone.

The Review queue (publication)

After you Accept, the drafted section lands here as a pending proposal. Review it as textbook text: does it fit the page, does it say only what the source supports, is it written for a student reader?

  • Publish → it becomes part of the textbook every student reads. The moment of actual consequence in the whole pipeline.
  • Reject → the proposal dies; the student's accepted status is unaffected (their scholarship was fine; the drafting wasn't).

Unsure? Leave it pending and flag Norm. A pending proposal costs nothing; a bad paragraph published into the textbook costs trust.

Error reports (the second tab of your Student queue)

Students earn participation credit for the first verified report of a genuine error in any Guide page. Open the page, open the source the page cites, and record what each actually says. If the student is right, verifying it opens a new gap on the board — and the reporter is told their claim on it counts toward their three articles. Reading skeptically pays; you make that promise real.

Rules that are rules

  1. Never assume the badge is right on a green. Spot-check. The failure direction that matters is false reassurance.
  2. Never resolve a disagreement by editing the page directly. Everything flows through the queues so the history stays honest.
  3. Don't touch gap statuses or claims in any other tool. If something seems stuck, screenshot it and flag Norm rather than working around it.
  4. Deadlines are automatic. Full credit, then a further week at full credit, then a 75% tier — the system grants the extensions. Don't promise exceptions.
  5. Tone in every note: students are writing a real textbook next year's class will study from. Treat submissions as manuscripts from junior colleagues.
