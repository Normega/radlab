Part one

What counts as a substance problem

The framework

One definition, nine substance classes

Substance use disorder: continued use despite substance-related problems — with as few as two indicators from a standard list sufficing, across alcohol, cannabis, opioids, stimulants, sedatives, hallucinogens, inhalants, tobacco and caffeine.

  • Tolerance — escalating dose for the same effect
  • Withdrawal — a substance-specific syndrome on stopping
  • Loss of control, failed cut-downs, time consumed, roles abandoned, use despite harm…

Note what the definition does not mention: quantity. The syndrome is defined by the relationship to the substance, not the dose.

Two indicators is a low bar and students should notice that — it returns in the caffeine slide as a live argument.

The mechanism, and its honest limits

The reward account

The mesocorticolimbic dopamine pathway, with opioid peptides, GABA, serotonin and endocannabinoids in supporting roles: substances hijack the machinery that reinforces survival behaviour.

Your Guide flags its own source here: the review names the pathways mostly through the drugs that act on them, cites no human imaging or lesion evidence, and is internally inconsistent on the direction of neurotransmitter change.

The behavioural account needs no such caution: positive reinforcement while intoxicated, negative reinforcement twice over — relief of distress, and relief of withdrawal. Withdrawal makes stopping punishing and use reinforcing.

Lecture 1's operant machinery carrying a whole chapter. The withdrawal-as-negative-reinforcement loop is the third self-sustaining cycle of the term — invite the room to name the other two (panic, insomnia).

The claim to retire

"Once an addict, always an addict"

Lifetime remission rates, by substance:

99.2%

cocaine

97.2%

cannabis

90.6%

alcohol

83.7%

nicotine

Relapse risk is hyperbolic — steep early, then falling. These numbers are hard to reconcile with "chronic relapsing brain disease" as a life sentence.

The most hopeful evidence in the course, and the least known. Most people with substance use disorders get better, most without formal treatment. Say it twice.

Canada, now

The toxic drug crisis in four numbers

  • 56,631 opioid toxicity deaths since 2016 (national surveillance)
  • Deaths falling 23% — while EMS responses rise 9%
  • 70% of deaths involve more than one substance

Read the middle pair together: fewer deaths, more emergencies. Something is keeping more overdoses from becoming deaths — which is what naloxone distribution and supervised consumption are for.

The falling-deaths/rising-EMS divergence is the analytically interesting fact — a measurement story, not just a tragedy story. It sets up Insite after the break.

Part two

Treatment — and the argument about what treatment is

What works best is barely used

Contingency management

Vouchers or prizes for verified abstinence. Operant conditioning, applied with a straight face.

It is the best-supported psychosocial treatment in this literature — and it is rarely disseminated.

Why? Paying people not to use offends intuitions about virtue. The evidence and the intuition point in opposite directions, and the intuition is winning.

Ask the room why it feels wrong before giving the dissemination fact. This is the Rush/tranquilizing-chair question from L1 pointed at ourselves.

The debate with a body count

Methadone: treatment or substitution?

The objection: an agonist maintains dependence. One drug has been swapped for another.

The reply: maintenance stabilises life, cuts overdose death and disease transmission, permits eventual taper — and the dependence that remains is to a measured, legal, survivable dose.

The Canadian guideline goes further: buprenorphine–naloxone first-line, strong recommendation, high-quality evidence — and withdrawal management alone should be avoided, because detox without maintenance elevates death risk.

"Detox alone is contraindicated" is the guideline fact students least expect. GRADE columns from L2 doing real work.

Harm reduction, adjudicated

Insite went to the Supreme Court

Canada's first supervised injection site. The federal government moved to close it.

9–0.

The Court held that closing Insite would contravene fundamental justice — citing evidence of increased treatment uptake and no increase in crime.

Note what settled it: not a change of values, but a body of evidence a court could weigh. This is what it looks like when data wins an argument.

A category on the move

Gambling sits with the substances now

DSM-5 moved gambling disorder into the addictions chapter — the first behaviour to be classified with the substances.

  • Same reward-pathway story, same tolerance-like escalation, same relief loop
  • Internet gaming disorder waits in Section III — "conditions for further study" — while ICD-11 already lists it

Forty years ago gambling was an impulse-control problem; today it is an addiction. Same behaviour, re-theorised — a category being argued about in real time.

Callback to PMDD's promotion (L5) and the OCD chapter's assembly (L3): the manual's edges move, and students have now watched it happen three times.

Part three

Impulse control

The chapter of outward expression

DSM chapter 15, reunited

Problems expressed outward — behaviour that violates others' rights or collides with social norms.

Intermittent explosive disorderrecurrent outbursts wildly out of proportion to provocation
Kleptomaniatension → theft of unneeded objects → relief
Pyromaniathe same loop, with fire
Oppositional defiant disorderangry, argumentative, vindictive — ≥4 of 8 symptoms, 6 months
Conduct disorderthe severe end: aggression, destruction, theft, rule violation

Note the tension-relief loop appearing again in kleptomania and pyromania — the chapter's kinship with the addictions is visible in its structure.

Where the evidence runs out

Pyromania barely exists in the literature

Fire-setting the behaviour: about 1% lifetime, strongly associated with antisocial personality.

Pyromania the diagnosis: the literature search returned essentially nothing.

And kleptomania's pharmacology is four neurotransmitter systems with no integrated model — SSRIs paradoxical, naltrexone effective, nobody knows quite why.

These are the chapters where your Guide's declared gaps run thickest — and where a well-sourced student contribution genuinely moves the state of the course's knowledge.
