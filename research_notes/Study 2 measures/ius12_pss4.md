## 1. Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale, short form (IUS-12)

Carleton, Norton, & Asmundson (2007). Fearing the unknown: A short version of
the Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale. *Journal of Anxiety Disorders, 21*(1), 105-117.
doi:10.1016/j.janxdis.2006.03.014 (PMID 16647833).

### Instructions (lab form)

> Please circle the number that best corresponds to how much you agree with each item.

This sentence is identical in the Vig and Hahn theses. Thibodeau's version has the same sentence with no
final full stop.

### Response scale

There are 5 points. Each label is exactly as printed on the lab form (Vig, Appendix F; Thibodeau and
Hahn print the same labels as column headers in title case):

| Value | Label |
|---|---|
| 1 | not at all characteristic of me |
| 2 | a little characteristic of me |
| 3 | somewhat characteristic of me |
| 4 | very characteristic of me |
| 5 | entirely characteristic of me |

Carleton-authored papers describe the scale only by its endpoints: "a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1
(not at all characteristic of me) to 5 (entirely characteristic of me)" (Carleton et al. 2012; Angehrn,
Krakauer & Carleton 2020).

### Items (lab-form order; none reverse-keyed)

The subscale is shown in brackets after each item: P = Prospective Anxiety, I = Inhibitory Anxiety.

1. Unforeseen events upset me greatly. [P]
2. It frustrates me not having all the information I need. [P]
3. Uncertainty keeps me from living a full life. [I]
4. One should always look ahead so as to avoid surprises. [P]
5. A small unforeseen event can spoil everything, even with the best of planning. [P]
6. When it's time to act, uncertainty paralyses me. [I]
7. When I am uncertain I can't function very well. [I]
8. I always want to know what the future has in store for me. [P]
9. I can't stand being taken by surprise. [P]
10. The smallest doubt can stop me from acting. [I]
11. I should be able to organize everything in advance. [P]
12. I must get away from all uncertain situations. [I]

No item is marked (R), because none is reverse-keyed (see Scoring).

The lab order is the relative order these items have in the 27-item IUS (Dugas Lab form). The 27-item
numbers are 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 15, 18, 19, 20, 21 and 25 respectively, checked against the UQO PDF.

### Scoring

- **Total score:** the sum of all 12 items, giving a range of 12-60. Higher scores mean greater intolerance
  of uncertainty. Sources: Korol et al. (2021, with Asmundson and Carleton), PMC7903861, "Total scores can
  range from 12 to 60, with higher scores indicating higher levels of IU"; Huang et al. (2019, with
  Carleton), PMC6863525.
- **Prospective Anxiety** (also called prospective IU): 7 items. In the lab-form numbering these are
  **1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11**, with a range of 7-35.
- **Inhibitory Anxiety** (also called inhibitory IU): 5 items. In the lab-form numbering these are
  **3, 6, 7, 10, 12**, with a range of 5-25.
  - Sources for membership: Lauriola, Mosca & Carleton (2016), Table 1, where the seven prospective items
    load only on Prospective IU and the five inhibitory items only on Inhibitory IU; Jacoby et al. (2013),
    Table 1.
  - Sources for the 7/5 split and the subscale ranges: Kusec et al. (2024), PMC11092298, "Prospective
    Anxiety scores range from 7 to 35, and Inhibitory Anxiety scores range from 5 to 25".
  - The names "Prospective Anxiety" and "Inhibitory Anxiety" are attributed to Carleton et al. (2007) by
    later papers. The 2007 abstract itself says only "representing both anxious and avoidance components
    of intolerance of uncertainty".
- **Reverse-keyed items: none.** Every item is worded so that agreement means more intolerance of
  uncertainty. Every scoring description found sums the raw responses, and none names a reversed item.

### Licence and permission

- **No licence statement was found from the authors.** The ePROVIDE IUS-12 entry (last updated June 2024)
  lists the copyright as "No information". IUS-12 is not in Mapi's catalogue of instruments it distributes,
  and the page offers no download or licensing route.
- The parent 27-item IUS is posted openly by the Dugas Lab (UQO PDF above) with no permission terms
  printed on it.
- **Not verified:** whether the 2007 article states any terms of use, or whether it reproduces the full
  scale. A secondary site (bio-protocol.org/bio101/r9488220) claims the authors published the full
  questionnaire in the article; that claim was not checked against the article.
- **Practical reading:** the scale is widely reproduced in theses and articles without any cited permission,
  but no source states "free for non-commercial research without permission". If written assurance is
  needed, ask Dr. Carleton (University of Regina).

### Discrepancies between sources

1. **Item order.** The lab form (all three theses) uses the order above. Jacoby et al. (2013) and many
   later papers renumber the scale with the prospective items first:
   - 1 Unforeseen, 2 frustrates, 3 look ahead, 4 small unforeseen, 5 future, 6 surprise, 7 organize
   - then 8 full life, 9 paralyses, 10 can't function, 11 smallest doubt, 12 get away

   Under that numbering Prospective = 1-7 and Inhibitory = 8-12. **Subscale keys are therefore
   order-specific**, so make sure the item order you administer matches the key you score with. The 2007
   article's own order could not be checked.
2. **"paralyses" / "paralyzes".** The lab form, the 27-item Dugas form and Lauriola use "paralyses".
   Jacoby (2013) uses "paralyzes".
3. **Item 5 comma.** The lab form and Dugas use "A small unforeseen event can spoil everything". Jacoby
   uses "A small, unforeseen event can spoil everything". Lauriola's English gloss, a back-translation,
   reads "even with the best planning", without "of".
4. **Item 7 comma.** The lab form, Dugas and Jacoby use "When I am uncertain I can't function very well".
   Lauriola's gloss adds a comma after "uncertain".
5. **Item 1 typo in a secondary source.** Jacoby's Method text quotes "Uncertain events upset me greatly",
   but its Table 1 gives "Unforeseen events upset me greatly".
6. **Instructions.** The lab IUS-12 form uses one sentence ("Please circle the number that best
   corresponds to how much you agree with each item."). The 27-item Dugas form reads: "You will find below
   a series of statements which describe how people may react to the uncertainties of life. Please use the
   scale below to describe to what extent each item is characteristic of you. Please circle a number (1 to
   5) that describes you best." PhenX uses that same 27-item text for the IUS. Some IUS-12 web
   reproductions use the 27-item text.
7. **Anchor labels.** The lab form labels all five points. The Dugas 27-item form labels only 1 ("Not at
   all characteristic of me"), 3 ("Somewhat characteristic of me") and 5 ("Entirely characteristic of
   me"). One secondary source, a Liverpool accepted manuscript, wrongly gives the top anchor as "Very
   Characteristic of me".

**Which version is the authors' own:** the lab form above, used in theses supervised by Asmundson and
Carleton. The wording also matches the developers' parent scale (Dugas Lab). It could not be compared
against the 2007 article itself.

## 2. Perceived Stress Scale, 4-item (PSS-4)

Cohen, S., Kamarck, T., & Mermelstein, R. (1983). A global measure of perceived stress. *Journal of
Health and Social Behavior, 24*, 385-396. Also Cohen, S., & Williamson, G. (1988). Perceived stress in a
probability sample of the United States. In S. Spacapan & S. Oskamp (Eds.), *The social psychology of
health*. Newbury Park, CA: Sage.

### Which items make up the PSS-4

- Cohen et al. 1983, p. 392 [read from scan]: "a short version of the scale, consisting of the four items
  (numbers 2, 6, 7, and 14) that were correlated most highly with the 14-item scale, was employed."
- Cohen & Williamson 1988, p. 46 [read from scan]: "a four-item scale consisting of items 2, 6, 7, and 14".

### Instructions (lab PSS-4 form, verbatim)

> The questions in this scale ask you about your feelings and thoughts during the last month. In each
> case, please indicate your response by placing an "X" over the circle representing HOW OFTEN you felt
> or thought a certain way.

The 1983 article's instructions (Appendix A) are longer [read from scan]:

> The questions in this scale ask you about your feelings and thoughts during the last month. In each
> case, you will be asked to indicate *how often* you felt or thought a certain way. Although some of the
> questions are similar, there are differences between them and you should treat each one as a separate
> question. The best approach is to answer each question fairly quickly. That is, don't try to count up
> the number of times you felt a particular way, but rather indicate the alternative that seems like a
> reasonable estimate.
> For each question choose from the following alternatives:

The lab's PSS-14 form uses this longer text; the lab's PSS-10 and PSS-4 forms use the short text.

### Response options (lab PSS-4 form)

| Value | Label |
|---|---|
| 0 | Never |
| 1 | Almost Never |
| 2 | Sometimes |
| 3 | Fairly Often |
| 4 | Very Often |

Other sources print the same labels with different capitalisation:
- Cohen et al. 1983: "0. never / 1. almost never / 2. sometimes / 3. fairly often / 4. very often".
- Lab scoring document: "0 = Never; 1 = Almost Never; 2 = Sometimes; 3 = Fairly often; 4 = Very often".

### Items (verbatim, lab PSS-4 form)

1. In the last month, how often have you felt that you were unable to control the important things in your life?
2. In the last month, how often have you felt confident about your ability to handle your personal problems? (R)
3. In the last month, how often have you felt that things were going your way? (R)
4. In the last month, how often have you felt difficulties were piling up so high that you could not overcome them?

These correspond to PSS-14 items 2, 6, 7 and 14. The wording is identical in Cohen et al. 1983, Appendix
A [read from scan]. That appendix marks items 6 and 7 with the footnote "Scored in the reverse
direction."

### Scoring

- From the lab scoring page: "PSS-4 scores are obtained by reverse coding items # 2 and 3."
- To reverse an item, recode 0=4, 1=3, 2=2, 3=1, 4=0. This is the lab's own example on the same page, where
  it describes the PSS-10 as "reversing the scores ... e.g., 0=4, 1=3, 2=2, etc.".
- **Total** = item 1 + (4 − item 2) + (4 − item 3) + item 4. The range is 0-16; higher scores mean more
  perceived stress.
- There are no subscales.
- From the lab scales page and FAQ: "The Perceived Stress Scale is not a diagnostic instrument; there are
  no score cut-offs."
- Context from Cohen & Williamson 1988, p. 46, for the 1983 US probability sample: PSS-4 mean 4.49, SD 2.96,
  alpha .60. Cohen et al. 1983, p. 392, reports a 4-item alpha of .72 and two-month test-retest r = .55.

### Licence and permission: the claim does not hold as stated

The claim was that Cohen's lab says the PSS is free for non-profit academic research. What the sources
actually say:

- **Lab FAQ (updated 2014).** It does not contain a blanket statement that the PSS is free for non-profit
  academic research. The closest wording is:
  - On translation: "YES, if it's for research or educational (non-profit) purposes."
  - On commercial use: "Use of the PSS in profit making ventures including corporate clinical trials
    requires special permission and a nominal charge."
- **The lab's current scales page** no longer handles permissions itself. It now says: "Please submit
  permission requests to use the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS) through the ePROVIDE^TM platform of MAPI
  RESEARCH TRUST … Submitting a request for permission to use the PSS is completely free of charge … It
  does not commit you to purchasing a questionnaire." The Common Cold Project page carries the same text.
- **ePROVIDE PSS-4 entry (July 2026):**
  - "© Copyright RST Assessments, LLC. All Rights Reserved (2022)"; "Distributed by Mapi Research Trust".
  - "Students, physicians, clinical practice, not-funded academic users: You may access the questionnaire
    and/or its available translations directly".
  - "Funded academic users, healthcare organizations, commercial users & IT companies: Fees may apply to
    your project. Submit your request".
  - For **electronic versions**: "A license agreement must be completed by all users and a user fee is
    required from commercial & funded academic users."
  - It also says e-versions follow a PSS-4 "e-Booklet", with screenshot-review options.

**What this means for Study 2:** the PSS is free without formality only for not-funded academic use.
Administering it on the web means a licence agreement must be completed via ePROVIDE whatever the funding
status. A funded study may also be charged a fee, which Mapi decides case by case. Submitting the request
is free.

### Discrepancies between sources

1. **Instructions.** The 1983 published version is longer, and its "*how often*" is in italics. The lab's
   PSS-4 and PSS-10 forms use a short version that adds the paper-specific "placing an 'X' over the circle"
   and puts HOW OFTEN in capitals. Neither version is written for web use. ePROVIDE's e-Booklet would set
   the electronic wording, but it was not accessible without an account.
2. **Anchor capitalisation** differs between the form, the 1983 article and the scoring document (shown
   above); the wording is the same.
3. **Item wording in the PSS-4: no differences.** All four items match word for word between the 1983
   appendix and the lab form.
4. **Elsewhere in the PSS family (does not affect the PSS-4):**
   - PSS-14 item 4 is "dealt successfully with irritating life hassles?" in the 1983 article but "dealt
     successfully with day to day problems and annoyances?" on the lab's PSS-14 form.
   - The "angered" item reads "things that happened that were outside of your control" in the PSS-14 and
     "things that were outside your control" in the PSS-10.
5. **The lab's "html version" link for the PSS-4** points to a third-party site (roadtowellbeing.ca), not
   to the lab. The Word form above is the lab's own.
6. **Copyright year.** Secondary sources repeat a "Copyright 1994, Sheldon Cohen" line. No copy of the lab's
   own PSS-4 form showed it: the lab's Word form contains only a header image, which was not readable as
   text. The current copyright holder of record on ePROVIDE is RST Assessments, LLC (2022).

**Which version is the authors' own:** the CMU lab's PSS-4 Word form. Its items are identical to Cohen et
al. (1983); its instructions are the lab's short form of the 1983 instructions.
