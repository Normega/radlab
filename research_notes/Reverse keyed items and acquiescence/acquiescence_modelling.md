# Controlling and modelling acquiescence response style (ARS) in scale development and analysis

Context for the report writer: the target instrument is a new agree-disagree scale, 34 positively keyed (PK) items, 6-point with no midpoint, per-item "Prefer not to answer"; EFA then CFA; then used as an RCT outcome. The lab is considering adding ~8-10 reverse/negatively keyed (NK) items. Notes below are organised by the six key questions. Full-text sources actually read in this session are marked [read]; abstract-only sources are marked [abstract]; claims from my own background knowledge that I could not verify in this session are marked [UNVERIFIED].

## Q1. Design: fully vs partially balanced scales; how many reverse-keyed items, and how to distribute them

### Takeaway
Every ARS-correction method needs *contradiction* in the data, i.e. some items keyed in the opposite direction; a fully unbalanced (all-PK) scale cannot separate ARS from content at all. Full balance is the gold standard, but partially balanced designs (e.g. one-third NK) are workable if analysed with a random-intercept model, which is far more robust to imbalance than the Ferrando/Lorenzo-Seva EFA-with-target approach. No source gives a hard minimum count of NK items; simulations that "work" have used about 33% NK items with at least 5 items per factor, and validity attenuation is notably worse below ~10 items per scale.

### Cited Findings
- **Identification needs contradiction.** "All methods designed to correct acquiescence bias rely on contradiction in survey responses. Without contradiction, it is logically impossible to identify which respondents are expressing agreement due to acquiescence"; in fully unbalanced scales "this contradiction does not exist, rendering acquiescence impossible to identify." The model can be *statistically* identified and still not *empirically* capture ARS. — Swatton, P. (2024). Agree to agree: correcting acquiescence bias in the case of fully unbalanced scales... *Quality & Quantity*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11135-024-01891-0 [read] — [Essex repository PDF](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf)
- **Theoretical minimum of one opposite-keyed item; practical minimum unknown.** The person/random-intercept approach "does not require a balanced scale to work... in theory only requires at least one opposite-worded indicator... How many additional indicators are required in practice will need to be discovered in future research." — [Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf) [read]
- **Plieninger & Heck (2018):** "any ARS model requires both regular and reversed items in order to disentangle ARS and the target trait"; "disentangling the parameters [ARS and trait] will be more difficult the fewer reversed items are used." — Plieninger, H., & Heck, D. W. (2018). A new model for acquiescence at the interface of psychometrics and cognitive psychology. *Multivariate Behavioral Research, 53*(5), 633-654. https://doi.org/10.1080/00273171.2018.1469966 — [accepted manuscript](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/49190/1/Plieninger-Heck_2018_A-new-model-for-Acquiescence.pdf) [read, intro]
- **Billiet & McClendon (2000)** set conditions for concluding that an agreement-ARS factor exists: a *common style factor behind at least two independent theoretical concepts, each measured by a balanced set of items* (loadings constrained equal). — Billiet, J. B., & McClendon, M. J. (2000). Modeling acquiescence in measurement models for two balanced sets of items. *Structural Equation Modeling, 7*(4), 608-628. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15328007SEM0704_5 — [abstract via Scinapse](https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2014001227) [abstract]
- **Ferrando, Lorenzo-Seva & Chico (2003)** procedure assumes a *balanced, theoretically unidimensional* scale: ARS is extracted as a second factor orthogonal to content via target rotation, then individual trait scores are corrected. — Ferrando, P. J., Lorenzo-Seva, U., & Chico, E. (2003). *Multivariate Behavioral Research, 38*(3), 353-374. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327906MBR3803_04 — as described in [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021, Psicothema](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf) [read]
- **Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando (2009)** extend this to *partially balanced multidimensional* scales. They select a **balanced subset ("core")**: if there are K NK items, K PK items are paired with them. ARS loadings are approximated from that balanced subset, three factors are extracted and rotated to a partially specified orthogonal target, and the resulting transformation is applied to the full item set. The approximation works best with more items, higher ARS loadings, lower residual variances, and **equal mean substantive loadings of PK and NK items within the balanced core**. — Lorenzo-Seva, U., & Ferrando, P. J. (2009). Acquiescent responding in partially balanced multidimensional scales. *British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology, 62*(2), 319-326. https://doi.org/10.1348/000711007X265164 — procedure as described in [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [read]
- **Earlier fully balanced multidimensional EFA method:** Lorenzo-Seva, U., & Rodríguez-Fornells, A. (2006). Acquiescent responding in balanced multidimensional scales and exploratory factor analysis. *Psychometrika, 71*(4), 769-777. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11336-004-1207-4 — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/psycho/v71y2006i4p769-777.html) [citation only]
- **Partial balance simulation (de la Fuente & Abad 2020):** 2 substantive factors + ARS; **66% PK / 34% NK in every condition**; 5, 8 or 12 items per factor; N = 200/500/1000; ARS loadings .15 or .30; NK substantive loadings .70 / .60 / .50 (full / medium / low "balance" against PK loadings of .70); 486 conditions. Random-intercept FA (RIFA, in ESEM form) reached congruence > .95 in 83% of conditions (mean .978) and beat EFA-target in 78.8% of conditions; EFA-target reached > .95 in 62.8% (mean .948). With low balance, RMSE of ARS loadings was EFA .174 vs RIFA .062, and RMSE of the factor correlation was .090 vs .047. Correlation of estimated with true factor scores was RIFA .922 vs EFA .908 (low balance: .902 vs .862). **Number of items per factor had essentially no effect on the EFA method's ARS recovery (ω²p = .004).** Authors expect that "a larger unbalance of the number of direct and reverse items might have more detrimental effects on the RIFA model", and they did not test it. — de la Fuente, J., & Abad, F. J. (2020). Comparing methods for modeling acquiescence in multidimensional partially balanced scales. *Psicothema, 32*(4), 590-597. https://doi.org/10.7334/psicothema2020.96 — [PDF](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [read]
- **Realistic NK item behaviour:** NK items tend to have *lower* substantive loadings (Weijters & Baumgartner 2012) and *higher* ARS loadings because they are longer and syntactically more complex (Condon, Ferrando & Demestre 2006, *PAID 40*, 403-407). This is exactly the "low balance" condition that degrades the EFA-target method. — [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [read]
- **Balance and external validity (Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021):** analytic derivation plus simulation (360 conditions × 100 reps; 4/10/16/30/50 items; balanced / quasi-balanced / unbalanced). "When the scales were well balanced, the controlled and uncorrected scores were close to each other, and led to unbiased validity estimates. When the scales were unbalanced and no corrections were used, attenuated empirical validity coefficients inevitably appeared." Number of items (η² = .383) and loading size (η² = .136) were the largest drivers of attenuation; "Validity decreases when there are fewer than 10 items, when the loadings of the substantive factor are low, and when the scale is unbalanced." Authors "strongly suggest that great care be taken when designing the measuring instrument" rather than relying solely on post-hoc correction. (Quasi-balanced was operationalised as equal numbers of PK/NK items but unequal loadings: .75 vs -.65 high, .55 vs -.45 low.) — Hernández-Dorado, A., Vigil-Colet, A., Lorenzo-Seva, U., & Ferrando, P. J. (2021). Is correcting for acquiescence increasing the external validity of personality test scores? *Psicothema, 33*(4), 639-646. https://doi.org/10.7334/psicothema2021.131 — [PDF](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf) [read]
- **Balance alone does not clean the covariance structure.** Including NK items "does not ensure that the means and covariance structure are free from acquiescence bias" (Vigil-Colet, Navarro-González & Morales-Vives 2020, *Psicothema 32*(1), 108-114), and NK items are controversial (Suárez-Álvarez et al. 2018, "Using reversed items in Likert scales: A questionable practice", *Psicothema 30*(2), 149-158). They can produce wording/method factors (DiStefano & Motl 2006; Weijters & Baumgartner 2012; Weijters, Baumgartner & Schillewaert 2013, *Psychological Methods 18*(3), 320-334). — cited in [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [read]
- **Balance helps even when the analyst does nothing.** Swatton notes that in a balanced scale ARS "cancels out" in the sum only approximately: "the sum of the acquiescence loadings on the items in one direction... should be roughly equal to the sum of the acquiescence loadings on the items in the other direction." This is more likely with more items, or can be engineered by piloting and selecting items. — [Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf) [read]
- **Tau-equivalence of ARS across items is questionable.** Kam & Zhou (2015) used an external acquiescence criterion and found that items are *not* equally affected by ARS. They propose an "acquiescence × item interaction" framework. — Kam, C. C. S., & Zhou, M. (2015). Does acquiescence affect individual items consistently? *Educational and Psychological Measurement, 75*(5), 764-784. https://doi.org/10.1177/0013164414560817 — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC5965517&resultType=core&format=json) [abstract]

### Inferences
- Adding 8-10 NK items to 34 PK items gives about 19-23% NK, which is *less* balanced than the 34% NK simulated by de la Fuente & Abad (2020). That design sits outside the tested range. RIFA is likely still usable, but there is no simulation evidence for that exact ratio, and the authors themselves warned that greater imbalance could hurt RIFA.
- Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando's (2009) EFA method uses only a balanced *core* (here 8-10 NK items paired with 8-10 PK items) to locate the ARS direction. Pairing PK and NK items of similar expected substantive loading, within the same facet, satisfies its key assumption. Placing NK items in only one or two facets would confound ARS with those facets' content in the core.
- Distribute NK items across facets, ideally at least 2 per facet if facets are expected to be retained (an inference from the "each factor needs ≥3 items" EFA convention and from the CTC(M-1)/RIFA logic that the ARS factor must cut across content). Write true opposite-pole items (e.g., "I rarely notice X") rather than negations ("I do not notice X"), because negated items are the main source of misresponse and wording factors (Weijters & Baumgartner 2012, cited above).
- Because validity attenuation rises sharply below ~10 items and with low loadings (Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021), short facet subscales (≤5 items) used as RCT outcomes are the most exposed to ARS.

### Gaps
- No source found states an empirically validated minimum *number* or *proportion* of NK items needed to identify an ARS factor or to compute a stable ARS index. Swatton (2024) explicitly says this is unknown.
- I could not retrieve full texts of Ferrando et al. (2003), Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando (2009) or Billiet & McClendon (2000) themselves; their procedures are described here via secondary accounts (de la Fuente & Abad 2020; Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021, 2025; Swatton 2024).
- Aichholzer (2014) and Kuru & Pasek (2016) do not give design recommendations on NK counts in the material I found.

## Q2. Statistical models: strengths, limitations, software

### Takeaway
Across three independent simulation programmes (Savalei & Falk 2014; de la Fuente & Abad 2020; Primi et al. 2019), the **random-intercept factor model (RIFA / RI-EFA / "person-intercept" CFA)** comes out as the best accuracy-to-simplicity trade-off. It adds one parameter (the ARS variance), works on unrecoded items, runs in standard SEM software (lavaan, Mplus), and is robust to moderate imbalance and to non-equal ARS loadings. Its main weakness is strongly heterogeneous ARS loadings. EFA-with-target (Ferrando group; FACTOR, vampyr, siren) is preferable only when balance is good and per-item ARS loadings are of interest. Ipsatisation and mean-partialling are simple but need full balance. IRTree/MPT models separate ARS from extreme and midpoint styles but need more items and Bayesian or custom estimation.

### Cited Findings
**Random intercept item factor analysis (RIFA)**
- Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman (2006) relax the fixed-intercept assumption so that intercepts vary across persons. This is "attractive when m factors are expected on the basis of substantive theory but m + 1 factors are needed in practice". It can be fitted in conventional SEM software and was compared with bifactor and CTC(M-1) models on an optimism dataset. — Maydeu-Olivares, A., & Coffman, D. L. (2006). Random intercept item factor analysis. *Psychological Methods, 11*(4), 344-362. https://doi.org/10.1037/1082-989X.11.4.344 — [Academia.edu summary](https://www.academia.edu/475094/Random_intercept_item_factor_analysis) [abstract]
- A random intercept "is mathematically equivalent to adding an additional orthogonal acquiescence factor in which all the loadings on the factor are constrained to be equal and positive", with items left **un-recoded**. Its advantage is simplicity: "adding a single parameter, also allowing to test the hypothesis of variance nullity associated with the response style." Its main weakness is heterogeneity of ARS loadings (ω²p = .954 on ARS-loading RMSE). Under *high* ARS-loading heterogeneity, EFA-target outperformed RIFA. Implemented in lavaan; ESEM version: fit an unrestricted 2-factor echelon model plus RI, then target-rotate the substantive part. — [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [read]
- **Comment on CTC(M-1) interpretation:** Geiser et al. argued that the meaning of latent variables in the CTC(M-1) model, as applied by Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman, differs from what was assumed. — "On the meaning of the latent variables in the CT-C(M-1) model: A comment on Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman (2006)" — [USU Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/psych_facpub/1301) [title only; content not read]
- **Swatton (2024)** relabels the model "person-intercept CFA". He argues the orthogonality of the ARS factor with content is not required for identification (it is an assumption made for comparison), drops it, and fits continuous and ordinal variants (CFA1/CFA2/OCFA1/OCFA2). — [Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf) [read]
- **RI-EFA (Aichholzer 2014):** simple-structure CFAs of Big Five measures misfit; RI-EFA "captures acquiescence as well as the complex item-factor structure typical for personality measures"; demonstrated on German NEO-FFI and BFI. — Aichholzer, J. (2014). Random intercept EFA of personality scales. *Journal of Research in Personality, 53*, 1-4. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jrp.2014.07.001 — [PMC4251787](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4251787/) [abstract]

**Savalei & Falk (2014) — three-way comparison, unidimensional balanced scale**
- Compared (a) ipsatisation based on Chan & Bentler (1993), (b) CFA with an equal-loading ARS factor (Billiet & McClendon 2000; Mirowsky & Ross 1991), i.e. the RI model, and (c) EFA with target rotation (Ferrando et al. 2003). "The CFA method performs best overall and... is robust to the violation of its assumptions; the EFA and the CB approaches work well when their assumptions are strictly met, and the 'do nothing' approach can be surprisingly robust when the ACQ factor is not very strong." — Savalei, V., & Falk, C. F. (2014). Recovering substantive factor loadings in the presence of acquiescence bias: A comparison of three approaches. *Multivariate Behavioral Research, 49*(5), 407-424. https://doi.org/10.1080/00273171.2014.931800 — [Falk lab page (abstract, via search snippet)](https://www.psych.mcgill.ca/perpg/fac/falk/publication/savalei-recovering-2014-1) [abstract]

**Primi et al. (2019, 2020)**
- Compared (1) the classical method of partialling out the person mean across balanced items, (2) IRT differential person functioning (DPF), and (3) MIRT with a random intercept, using **N = 40,649 students aged 11-18 on a fully balanced 30-item conscientious self-management scale**. — Primi, R., Santos, D., De Fruyt, F., & John, O. P. (2019). Comparison of classical and modern methods for measuring and correcting for acquiescence. *British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology, 72*(3), 447-465. https://doi.org/10.1111/bmsp.12168 — [Psicothema reference list](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf); design details via [search result summary](https://connect.itf.edu.br/galeria/getImage/385/3034251672207548.pdf) [abstract-level only]
- Primi, De Fruyt et al. found differences in criterion-related validity between ARS-uncorrected and corrected scores, and found "false-keyed" items *more* valid than "true-keyed" items for predicting achievement. — Primi, R., De Fruyt, F., Santos, D., Antonoplis, S., & John, O. P. (2020; online 2019). True or false? Keying direction and acquiescence influence the validity of socio-emotional skills items in predicting high school achievement. *International Journal of Testing, 20*(2), 97-121. https://doi.org/10.1080/15305058.2019.1673398 — as summarised in [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf) [read, secondary]
- Several comparisons (de la Fuente & Abad 2020; Primi et al. 2019; Savalei & Falk 2014) have RIFA "usually emerg[ing] as the winner in terms of the acceptable overall performance-simplicity trade-off", though the Ferrando group adds that "this first place is due more to easiness of im[plementation]". — [Hernández-Dorado, Ferrando & Vigil-Colet 2025](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf) [read]

**Ferrando / Lorenzo-Seva family (unrestricted FA; FACTOR; vampyr; siren)**
- Ferrando, Lorenzo-Seva & Chico (2009) give a general factor-analytic procedure for ARS *and* social desirability together (SEM 16(2), 364-381, https://doi.org/10.1080/10705510902751374). Ferrando, Morales-Vives & Lorenzo-Seva (2016) handle ARS when ARS *correlates with content* (SEM 23(5), 713-725, https://doi.org/10.1080/10705511.2016.1185723). — cited in [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [read]
- Software: **vampyr** R package (Navarro-González, Vigil-Colet, Ferrando, Lorenzo-Seva & Tendeiro 2020, v1.1.1, "Factor analysis controlling the effects of response bias"); **Psychological Test Toolbox** (Navarro-González et al. 2019, *Journal of Statistical Software 91*(6)). — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021 references](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf) [read]
- **siren** R package (v1.0.6, March 2025; Navarro-González, Ferrando, Morales-Vives, Hernández-Dorado): hybrid EFA-CFA. Stage 1 estimates ARS loadings via an unrestricted FA with rotation, partials ARS out of the inter-item correlations, then fits the restricted CFA (options "fixed" or "resid"), with Pearson or polychoric correlations. Requires opposite-direction items and ≥3 items per content factor. Main function `acquihybrid()`. — [CRAN refman](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/siren/refman/siren.html) [read; fetch summary described stage 1 as "MRFA rotation" and the base reference as "Ferrando & Lorenzo-Seva (2000)", treat those two details as UNVERIFIED]
- The Ferrando-group Table of methods: ipsative method (Chan & Bentler 1993); general style factor model (Billiet & McClendon 2000); unrestricted FA with target rotation (Ferrando et al. 2003); RIFA (Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman 2006); partially balanced EFA (Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando 2009, adapting Lorenzo-Seva & Rodríguez-Fornells 2006); RI-EFA hybrid (Aichholzer 2014); SIREN hybrid. — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025, Table 1](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf) [read]
- **ARS and correlated residuals (doublets):** correcting for ARS when doublets exist makes the ARS factor *absorb* residual covariance. In a simulation, combined ARS + residual correction absorbed 100% of doublets. Without ARS correction, doublets between same-sign items were overestimated, and 23.5% of opposite-sign doublets were missed. Recommendation: a dual correction (SIREN for ARS + MORGANA for residuals), or detect residual correlations first when the number of factors is uncertain. Simulation limited to fully/essentially balanced 6-8 item sets. — Hernández-Dorado, A., Ferrando, P. J., & Vigil-Colet, A. (2025). The impact and consequences of correcting for acquiescence when correlated residuals are present. *Psicothema, 37*(1), 11-20. — [PDF](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf) [read]

**Method factors / correlated uniqueness / CTC(M-1)**
- Alternatives for wording artefacts: a method factor for the subset of NK (or PK) items (Tomás & Oliver 1999), correlated uniquenesses among same-direction items (Marsh 1996, *JPSP 70*(4), 810-819), or a general method factor affecting all items (Billiet & McClendon 2000). — [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [read]
- Distinction: in the ARS-as-RI model, PK and NK items load +1 on the ARS factor without recoding (equivalently +1/-1 after recoding), and ARS/disacquiescence are treated as poles of one dimension. Wording/method factors instead capture shared variance among NK items only. "Only few studies compared the two phenomena." Careless responding (missing negations) also inflates agreement but "acquiescence is not necessarily related to being inattentive" (Swain, Weathers & Niedrich 2008; Weijters & Baumgartner 2012; Weijters et al. 2013). — [Plieninger & Heck 2018](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/49190/1/Plieninger-Heck_2018_A-new-model-for-Acquiescence.pdf) [read]

**IRTree / MPT / mixture models**
- Böckenholt (2012) IR-tree: responses decomposed into binary pseudo-items (midpoint? / agree-disagree direction / extreme?), fitted as a multidimensional binary IRT model with standard ML software. It only covers MRS and ERS, and only trees where each category has a unique path. Construct validity of the processes: Plieninger & Meiser (2014). Multi-domain extension: Khorramdel & von Davier (2014). — [Plieninger & Heck 2018](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/49190/1/Plieninger-Heck_2018_A-new-model-for-Acquiescence.pdf) [read]
- Plieninger & Heck's "Acquiescence Model": with probability *a*, a respondent enters an acquiescent state that yields agreement regardless of keying; otherwise Böckenholt's tree applies. It is a mixture of trait-based and ARS-based agreement, estimated as a Bayesian hierarchical MPT (the paper reports unbiased recovery in simulation). It requires regular and reversed items, and needs ≥5 categories for identification as described (8 non-redundant category probabilities for 5 free parameters with 5-point items). — [Plieninger & Heck 2018](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/49190/1/Plieninger-Heck_2018_A-new-model-for-Acquiescence.pdf) [read]
- Three operational families of ARS measurement: (1) aggregate regular + reversed items without recoding, either as a manifest index for partialling (ten Berge 1999) or as an ARS factor (Billiet & McClendon; Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman; Mirowsky & Ross 1991); (2) pairs of logical opposites (Soto et al. 2008; Winkler, Kanouse & Ware 1982); (3) counting *agree-category* use, with content-heterogeneous items (Weijters et al. 2010b) or with homogeneous items (Falk & Cai 2016; Johnson & Bolt 2010; Wetzel & Carstensen 2017). "Empirical evidence of convergent validity is mixed." — [Plieninger & Heck 2018](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/49190/1/Plieninger-Heck_2018_A-new-model-for-Acquiescence.pdf) [read]

**Manifest vs latent comparison (2025)**
- The manifest approach (deviation from the scale median) offers "technical simplicity" but limited bias reduction. The latent approach (CFA bifactor-type ARS factor) "enables identification and meaningful reduction" of bias. — Havan, P., Halama, P., & Kohút, M. (2025). The tendency to agree with items regardless of content as a possible source of bias in social science research. *Sociologický časopis / Czech Sociological Review, 61*(3), 301-321. https://doi.org/10.13060/csr.2024.013 — [abstract page](https://sreviewdev.soc.cas.cz/artkey/csr-202503-0003_the-tendency-to-agree-with-items-in-social-survey-regardless-of-their-content-as-a-possible-source-of-bias.php) [abstract]

### Inferences
- Software mapping (my synthesis): **lavaan/Mplus** for RIFA/CFA (ARS factor with all loadings fixed to 1 on raw, unrecoded items, free variance, orthogonal to content). RI-EFA/ESEM is possible in Mplus and in lavaan's `efa()` blocks (lavaan ≥ 0.6-13) [UNVERIFIED version number]. Use **FACTOR** (Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando's standalone program), **vampyr** and **siren** for the Ferrando/Lorenzo-Seva unrestricted-FA corrections and ARS-corrected factor scores. IRTree pseudo-items can be fitted in mirt or lme4/glmer, and the Plieninger-Heck MPT model in Bayesian software (the paper used hierarchical MPT estimation) [package name not verified].
- For ordinal 6-point data, fit RIFA with WLSMV on polychorics (Mplus or lavaan `ordered=`). With "Prefer not to answer" treated as missing, WLSMV uses pairwise information. FIML requires ML/MLR on treated-as-continuous data or full-information categorical estimation. This is a practical choice the source papers did not address.

## Q3. Can ARS be estimated WITHOUT reverse-keyed items in the target scale?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only by importing contradiction from elsewhere. Options are (a) a separate set of balanced "marker" items or balanced scales from other constructs (Watson 1992; Billiet & McClendon's ≥2 balanced concepts; Swatton 2024), or (b) a response-style index from many content-heterogeneous items (Baumgartner & Steenkamp 2001; Weijters et al. 2008/2010's representative-indicators approach). Both rest on a strong extra assumption: that ARS is the same person-level tendency across content domains. The evidence partly supports this (cross-domain consistency is moderate to high), but item-level ARS is not uniform.

### Cited Findings
- **Watson (1992)**: correcting for ARS "in the absence of a balanced scale", applied to class consciousness. — Watson, D. (1992). Correcting for acquiescent response bias in the absence of a balanced scale. *Sociological Methods & Research, 21*(1), 52-88. https://doi.org/10.1177/0049124192021001003 — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/somere/v21y1992i1p52-88.html) [citation only]
- **Swatton (2024) implementation:** used two separate *balanced* scales in the same survey (zero-sum beliefs; personal empathy) to identify a person-intercept ARS factor that also loads on a fully unbalanced left-right scale (British Election Study Internet Panel wave 14). Correction moved results toward those of a comparable balanced scale. Caveat: this "necessarily strengthens the assumption, as it assumes not only the same level of acquiescence for each respondent on one scale, but on all scales in the model." Uncorrected unbalanced scales shifted means toward the direction of imbalance and could create "spurious... inflated... or hidden causal associations." — [Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf) [read]
- **Ferrando group:** "a priori" design methods include balanced scales (Ray 1979) "or the inclusion of specific ACQ scales (e.g. Watson 1992; Krosnick, 1999; Saris et al., 2010)"; but "they increase the complexity of the analyses, and there is no convincing evidence of their advantages in terms of ACQ control." — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf) [read]
- **Heterogeneous-item indices:** Baumgartner & Steenkamp (2001) measured ARS, DARS, ERS, MRS and noncontingent responding in representative samples from 11 EU countries. Effects on scale scores depended on (i) the proportion of reverse-scored items and (ii) how far the scale mean deviates from the scale midpoint. Correlations among four consumer scales of .15-.40 dropped to .00-.20 after controlling for response styles. — Baumgartner, H., & Steenkamp, J.-B. E. M. (2001). Response styles in marketing research: A cross-national investigation. *Journal of Marketing Research, 38*(2), 143-156. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.38.2.143.18840 — [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/response-styles-in-marketing-research-a-cross-national-investigat/) [abstract + secondary summary of the correlation example]
- **Weijters, Schillewaert & Geuens (2008)**, RIRSMACS (representative indicators response style means and covariance structure): response styles measured from randomly sampled, content-heterogeneous items and used to correct across paper, telephone and online modes. — *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 36*(3), 409-422. — [Vlerick repository](https://repository.vlerick.com/entities/publication/bdce5170-2e5c-489d-bdf5-ec5bb800644d) [abstract]
- **Weijters, Geuens & Schillewaert (2010)**: same respondents, two questionnaires of *independent random item samples*, 1 year apart. A second-order model with time-invariant and time-specific ARS/DARS/MRS/ERS factors showed "response styles have an important stable component, a small part of which can be explained by demographics." — *Psychological Methods, 15*(1), 96-110. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018721 — [IDEAS working paper](https://ideas.repec.org/p/rug/rugwps/08-547.html) [abstract]
- **Cross-domain consistency:** Danner et al. (2015) found ARS "consistent across different question types". De la Fuente & Abad summarise it as "medium to high correlations... between acquiescence factors obtained from personality and attitude scale items." — Danner, D., Aichholzer, J., & Rammstedt, B. (2015). Acquiescence in personality questionnaires: Relevance, domain specificity, and stability. *Journal of Research in Personality, 57*, 119-130. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jrp.2015.05.004 — [search abstract](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/54554/); [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [abstract + secondary]
- **Against the uniform-ARS assumption:** Kam & Zhou (2015) found that an external ARS criterion did not affect items equally. — [Kam & Zhou 2015](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC5965517&resultType=core&format=json) [abstract]
- **Item-format alternative (avoid A/D altogether):** Kuru & Pasek (2016) found that agree-disagree formats showed more ARS and less criterion validity than item-specific formats. Controlling for ARS showed it had inflated reliabilities and factor loadings of Facebook-use scales and altered correlations among measures and with covariates. A secondary source attributes to them an estimate that 10-20% of participants are affected. — Kuru, O., & Pasek, J. (2016). Improving social media measurement in surveys: Avoiding acquiescence bias in Facebook research. *Computers in Human Behavior, 57*, 82-92 [volume and pages UNVERIFIED]. — [ISR/UMich listing](https://api.isr.umich.edu/?p=192460); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquiescence_bias) [abstract + secondary; the 10-20% figure is from secondary sources only, UNVERIFIED]

### Inferences
- For the lab's scale, a feasible no-NK-in-target design is a short **balanced marker block** (e.g., 6-12 content-unrelated, logically opposite A/D pairs) administered in the same 6-point format. Specify a person-intercept/RI factor spanning both the marker block and the 34 PK items, with the marker block's balance supplying identification. This is Swatton's (2024) design applied to psychology. It assumes ARS is the same across blocks, which is plausible given Danner et al. (2015) and Weijters et al. (2010) but contradicted at the item level by Kam & Zhou (2015).
- A heterogeneous-item ARS index (proportion of agree responses across many unrelated items) can serve as a covariate, but it is confounded with true content unless the items are numerous and truly heterogeneous. That is the RIRSMACS rationale. It does not repair the factor structure of the target scale.
- Embedding NK items *within* the scale gives within-scale identification but costs wording-factor risk. A separate marker block avoids wording factors in the target scale but needs the cross-domain-invariance assumption. The lab could do both, and test whether ARS from markers and ARS from in-scale NK items correlate highly. That would be a direct check of the assumption.

### Gaps
- No simulation was found that evaluates how many marker items are needed for identification of an external ARS factor.
- Baumgartner & Steenkamp's recommended number of heterogeneous items per response-style index was not retrieved [I recall ~ "randomly selected items with low inter-item correlations" but cannot verify a number].

## Q4. Magnitude: how much ARS biases means, correlations, factor structure and group comparisons

### Takeaway
ARS typically accounts for only about 3-5% of item variance, but it is systematic. It inflates same-direction inter-item correlations and therefore reliability, biases correlations between scales (in either direction), shifts means of unbalanced scales, and degrades factor structure, especially in low-education, older, adolescent, and high-deference-culture samples. Its person-level stability is high (test-retest ~.6, with ~50-60% trait variance over 8 years).

### Cited Findings
- **Variance share:** "ACQ causes 3-5% of the variance in personality or attitude scales, and it can spuriously inflate inter-item correlations and, therefore, reliability estimates (Lechner et al., 2019)". — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf) [read, secondary]. "About 4% of the variance of personality items is due to ACQ (Danner et al., 2015)"; "about 6% of participants respond in a pronounced acquiescent or disacquiescent way (Hinz et al., 2007)". — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf) [read, secondary]; Hinz, A., Michalski, D., Schwarz, R., & Herzberg, P. Y. (2007). The acquiescence effect in responding to a questionnaire. *GMS Psycho-Social Medicine, 4*, 1-9 ([PMC2736523](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2736523/)).
- **Danner et al. (2015)**: German (N = 1,999) and Austrian (N = 3,266) representative samples, short Big Five inventory, SEM. ARS "systematically affects the variance of personality items and biases the association with other variables", is consistent across question types, and is moderately stable over time. Test-retest ≈ .6 over 2 months. — [abstract via search](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/54554/); test-retest figure from [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf)
- **Stability:** ≈ .6 over four years (Billiet & Davidov 2008, *Sociological Methods & Research 36*(4), 542-562) — [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf). Over 8 years, 49-59% of state ARS/ERS variance was trait variance (German panel, random item sets). — Wetzel, E., Lüdtke, O., Zettler, I., & Böhnke, J. R. (2016). The stability of extreme response style and acquiescence over 8 years. *Assessment, 23*(3), 279-291. https://doi.org/10.1177/1073191115583714 — [Tübingen repository](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/73876) [abstract]
- **Correlation bias:** between-scale correlations of .15-.40 fell to .00-.20 after response-style control (Baumgartner & Steenkamp 2001) — [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/response-styles-in-marketing-research-a-cross-national-investigat/) [secondary summary]. ARS "tends to increase the correlations between items that are worded in the same direction but are not conceptually related". It can bias content-loading invariance in comparative studies and stability or cross-lagged estimates in panels (Billiet & Davidov 2008). — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf) [read]
- **Bias magnitude depends on ARS-trait correlation:** Plieninger (2017) simulation: "bias may be substantial if the attribute of interest is correlated with response style, however, in the absence of such correlations, bias was generally very small." — Plieninger, H. (2017). Mountain or molehill? A simulation study on the impact of response styles. *Educational and Psychological Measurement, 77*(1), 32-53. https://doi.org/10.1177/0013164416636655 — [search summary](https://metatoc.com/papers/53500-mountain-or-molehill-a-simulation-study-on-the-impact-of-response-styles) [secondary; full abstract fetch timed out]
- **Factor structure:** Navarro-González et al. (2016), N = 532 adolescents (11-18), Big Five measure plus aggression measure. Without controlling ARS + social desirability, fit to the expected model worsened. Congruence with the expected structure dropped from .948 to .872 (personality) and from .931 to .475 (aggression). — Navarro-González, D., Lorenzo-Seva, U., & Vigil-Colet, A. (2016). How response bias affects the factorial structure of personality self-reports. *Psicothema, 28*(4), 465-470. https://doi.org/10.7334/psicothema2016.113 — [Uniovi record](https://reunido.uniovi.es/index.php/PST/article/view/11388) [abstract]. Also Morales-Vives, Lorenzo-Seva & Vigil-Colet (2017), *Anales de Psicología 33*(3), 589-596; Soto, John, Gosling & Potter (2008), *JPSP 94*(4), 718-737 (ARS distorts Big Five structure in youth). — cited in [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf)
- **Education:** Big Five structure emerges "in a blurry way" in lower-education samples and clearly in highly educated ones. "Strong individual differences in acquiescence... among less highly educated persons", and after controlling ARS "the Big-Five model holds at all educational levels." — Rammstedt, B., Goldberg, L. R., & Borg, I. (2010). The measurement equivalence of Big-Five factor markers for persons with different levels of education. *Journal of Research in Personality, 44*(1), 53-61. — [PMC2854544 / search summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2854544) [abstract]
- **Country and individual correlates:** ~40,000 respondents in 20 European countries. 15% of ARS variance was between countries (explained by corruption and collectivism); within countries, conservatism and educational attainment were predictors. — Rammstedt, B., Danner, D., & Bosnjak, M. (2017). Acquiescence response styles: A multilevel model explaining individual-level and country-level differences. *Personality and Individual Differences, 107*, 190-194. — [SSOAR](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/handle/document/58337) [abstract]. World Values Survey, 60 countries, N = 90,347: ARS higher for older respondents (>55), the less educated, those valuing conformity/tradition, and (unexpectedly) men; much higher in countries with stronger deference norms. — Lechner, C. M., Partsch, M. V., Danner, D., & Rammstedt, B. (2019). Individual, situational, and cultural correlates of acquiescent responding: Towards a unified conceptual framework. *BJMSP, 72*(3), 426-446. https://doi.org/10.1111/bmsp.12164 — [MADOC](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/54609) [abstract]
- **Validity attenuation:** with unbalanced, uncorrected scales, validity coefficients are inevitably attenuated. Correction gains are "very subtle but nontrivial", even for almost fully balanced scales. — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf) [read]
- **Means of unbalanced scales** shift in the direction of imbalance. In UK data, unbalanced vs balanced versions of the same constructs produced visibly different distributions and different regression results. — [Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf) [read]

### Inferences
- For a 34-item all-PK scale, ARS variance (a few %) adds coherently across items, so its share of *sum-score* variance can exceed its per-item share and will inflate α/ω. "Excellent" reliability in pilot data partly reflects ARS.
- Student samples are lower-risk than general-population or low-education samples. Online panels with older or less-educated respondents, or cross-cultural use, are higher-risk. If the RCT recruits broadly, education is a plausible ARS moderator and should be recorded.

### Gaps
- No pooled meta-analytic estimate of ARS variance specific to online panels (e.g., Prolific/MTurk) was found.
- Effects of the 6-point/no-midpoint format on ARS were not retrieved. Weijters, Cabooter & Schillewaert (2010), *IJRM 27*(3), 236-247, studied the number of categories and labelling effects on response styles, but I did not read their findings.

## Q5. Practical guidance for EFA when ARS is present

### Takeaway
In *balanced* (or substantially balanced) scales, a strong ARS often appears as an **extra factor**. Ignoring it, or extracting it and then rotating to simple structure, biases loadings and cross-loadings. Use an informed (target) rotation or RI-EFA. In *fully unbalanced* scales, ARS does not appear as a separate factor; it merges into the content/general factor, so loading patterns look fine while scores and correlations are contaminated. For EFA-then-CFA on a partially balanced scale, the evidence favours RI-EFA/ESEM (de la Fuente & Abad 2020) and, if local dependencies are suspected, residual screening before or alongside ARS control (Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025).

### Cited Findings
- **D'Urso et al. (2023)**: "acquiescence response style was often captured as an additional factor in balanced scales when strong. For these scales, ignoring extraction of this additional factor or rotating to simple structure when extracting it harmed the recovery of the original measurement model by introducing bias in loadings and cross-loadings. These issues were avoided by using informed rotation approaches where the rotation target is specified according to a priori expectations... Not extracting the additional ARS factor did not affect loading recovery in unbalanced scales." — D'Urso, E. D., Tijmstra, J., Vermunt, J. K., & De Roover, K. (2023). Awareness is bliss: How acquiescence affects exploratory factor analysis. *Educational and Psychological Measurement, 83*(3), 433-472. https://doi.org/10.1177/00131644221089857 — [Europe PMC abstract](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC10177316&resultType=core&format=json) [abstract]
- **Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman (2006)**: the RI model is for when theory says m factors but m + 1 are needed empirically. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/475094/Random_intercept_item_factor_analysis) [abstract]
- **Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando (2009) procedure** (balanced core, then ARS-informed orthogonal target, then target rotation of substantive factors) is "severely affected by a low degree of balance". RI-EFA is more robust except under highly heterogeneous ARS loadings. The EFA procedure is preferred when "the interest of the study focuses on the degree in which each item elicits acquiescence." — [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf) [read]
- **Worked example of the balanced procedure in FACTOR-style workflow:** a 6-item balanced Exam Anxiety subscale. Unidimensionality was checked (ECV = .861; I-REAL = .215; Optimal Implementation of Parallel Analysis → 1 factor), then the Ferrando et al. (2003) ARS-controlled bidimensional model was fitted. Content loadings were nearly unchanged (congruence .998 vs uncorrected), and validity improved only slightly. — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2021](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4713.pdf) [read]
- **Factor structure degradation from response bias:** congruence .948 → .872 (Big Five) and .931 → .475 (aggression) when ARS + SD were not controlled. — [Navarro-González et al. 2016](https://reunido.uniovi.es/index.php/PST/article/view/11388) [abstract]
- **Doublets interact with ARS correction:** an ARS factor fitted in the presence of correlated residuals absorbs them, biasing ARS loadings. If the number of factors is uncertain, first assess residual correlations (e.g., Christensen, Garrido & Golino 2023; partial-correlation/image approach, Ferrando et al. 2022), then estimate ARS. — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf) [read]
- **Wording factors vs ARS:** removing reverse-scored items improving fit (e.g., Rodebaugh et al. 2007) is "unsurprising" because ARS inflates reliabilities and correlations among same-direction items. Better fit after dropping NK items is not evidence that ARS is absent. — [Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf) [read]

### Inferences
- **Recommended EFA workflow for the lab's data** (synthesis):
  1. Analyse **unrecoded** responses (NK items not reversed) when fitting ARS models.
  2. Determine dimensionality with and without an RI. With ~20% NK items, expect a possible "NK wording" or ARS factor; do not interpret an extra factor loading mainly on NK items as a substantive facet.
  3. Fit RI-EFA/ESEM (Mplus, or lavaan with `efa()` blocks) with target rotation to the hypothesised facets. Compare with the Lorenzo-Seva & Ferrando (2009) / siren approach as a sensitivity check.
  4. Screen for doublets (near-duplicate items are common in 34-item pools) before finalising, since they bias ARS estimates.
  5. Carry the RI into the CFA, and test ARS variance > 0 (e.g., Δχ² or a CI on the variance).
- With an all-PK scale, EFA *will not* reveal ARS. A clean one- or few-factor solution with high loadings is not reassurance; it is the expected appearance of contamination.

### Gaps
- D'Urso et al. (2023) full simulation conditions and numeric thresholds (e.g., ARS strength at which parallel analysis adds a factor) were not retrieved (PMC blocked).
- Navarro-González et al. (2016) full text not read; whether the effect is attributable to ARS specifically or to ARS plus social desirability jointly is not separated in the abstract.

## Q6. Implications for an RCT where change scores are the outcome

### Takeaway
No source retrieved addresses ARS in RCT change scores directly; the points below are largely inference from stability evidence. Because ARS is a fairly stable person trait (≈.6 test-retest; ~50-60% trait variance over 8 years), its *stable* part partly cancels in within-person pre-post differences, and randomisation balances ARS across arms. The between-arm treatment effect on an all-PK scale is therefore not biased *unless* (a) the intervention itself changes agreement tendency or demand-driven "agreeing" (plausible when items echo the intervention's language), (b) ARS × time variance differs by arm, or (c) the occasion-specific ARS component (~40-50% of ARS variance) adds noise and lowers power. Balanced keying, or an external ARS marker measured at each wave, is what lets these be tested.

### Cited Findings
- ARS stability: ~.6 at 2 months (Danner et al. 2015) and ~.6 at 4 years (Billiet & Davidov 2008) — [de la Fuente & Abad 2020](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4639.pdf); 49-59% trait variance over 8 years — [Wetzel et al. 2016](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/73876); "important stable component" over 1 year — [Weijters et al. 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/p/rug/rugwps/08-547.html).
- In panel designs, ARS can "generate substantial bias in the estimation of stability coefficients and cross-lagged effects between variables over time" (Billiet & Davidov 2008). — [Hernández-Dorado et al. 2025](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4868.pdf) [read]
- Bias is substantial when the attribute is correlated with response style and "generally very small" otherwise. — [Plieninger 2017 summary](https://metatoc.com/papers/53500-mountain-or-molehill-a-simulation-study-on-the-impact-of-response-styles) [secondary]
- Intervention evaluations with self-report are subject to **response-shift bias**, where the intervention changes the respondent's internal standard. Retrospective pretests are one suggested remedy. — Howard et al. (1980), *Evaluation Review* — [SAGE record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0193841X8000400105) [abstract snippet only; author list UNVERIFIED]
- Without longitudinal measurement invariance, pre-post change scores may misrepresent change on the latent variable. — [Springer, Quality of Life Research article s11136-017-1500-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11136-017-1500-1) [search snippet only; title/authors not verified]
- Variables that "causally contribute to a respondent's level of acquiescence will appear to contribute to the scale" in unbalanced scales. This yields spurious, inflated or hidden associations. — [Swatton 2024](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39129/1/s11135-024-01891-0.pdf) [read]

### Inferences
- **Does ARS cancel in pre-post change?** Only partially. With an all-PK scale, observed change = true change + (ARS_post − ARS_pre) × Σ loadings. The stable ARS component cancels. The occasion-specific component (roughly 40-50% of ARS variance per Wetzel et al.) does not; it adds error variance to change scores and lowers power.
- **Is the treatment effect biased?** Randomisation makes baseline ARS equal in expectation across arms, so the ARS *level* does not bias the arm difference. Bias arises if the intervention shifts *agreement behaviour* itself: participants who learned the intervention's vocabulary, want to please researchers, or feel more "positive" agree more with PK self-descriptions. In an all-PK scale this is indistinguishable from true improvement. In a balanced or partially balanced scale, true improvement raises PK and lowers NK agreement, while ARS-type change raises both. That contrast is the key reason to include NK items in an outcome measure.
- **Analysis options:** (a) longitudinal RIFA with an ARS factor at each wave, testing whether ARS means or variances differ by arm and time; (b) include an external ARS marker block at each wave and use it as a time-varying covariate or to identify the RI; (c) report treatment effects on PK and NK subscores separately as a robustness check. Use ANCOVA with baseline as covariate, rather than raw change scores, as standard RCT practice; this also absorbs the stable ARS component through the baseline covariate [general methodological knowledge, not from the retrieved sources].
- Pre-register ARS handling (which model, what happens if ARS variance is ~0, how NK items are scored) so that "corrected" vs "uncorrected" effects are not chosen post hoc.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found that estimates ARS-induced bias in RCT treatment effects, or that tests whether an intervention changes ARS. This is a genuine gap in what I retrieved; targeted searches returned only stability and response-shift literature.
- Longitudinal invariance testing of an RI/ARS factor across arms was not found in a retrieved source.
