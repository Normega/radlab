## Q1. FFMQ: do the negatively worded facets (Non-judging, Acting with Awareness) show DIF between meditators and non-meditators, or change meaning after training?

### Takeaway
Yes, on both counts. In the FFMQ, keying direction is confounded with facet: Acting with Awareness and Non-judging are (almost) entirely negatively worded. Meditators and non-meditators with the *same* latent mindfulness answer positive and negative items differently: 18 of 39 items showed DIF. Positive and negative wording forms substantive method factors, and the factor structure itself fails configural invariance from before to after MBCT. Acting with Awareness is the facet that most often fails strong invariance over treatment. It is also the facet with the weakest specific reliability once a general factor is modelled. A pre–post mean change on mixed-keyed mindfulness scales therefore cannot be assumed to be change in the construct.

### Cited Findings
- **Van Dam, Earleywine & Danoff-Burg (2009)**, *Personality and Individual Differences*. In an online sample, meditators and non-meditators *with similar overall levels of mindfulness* endorsed response options differently for positively vs negatively worded items. DIF was reported for **18 of the 39** FFMQ items. Meditators were more likely to *endorse positively worded* items; non-meditators were more likely to *deny negatively worded (reverse-scored)* items. — [Univ. Melbourne record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1220506-differential-item-function-across-meditators-and-non-meditators-on-the-five-facet-mindfulness-questionnaire); summary of the "18 of 39" figure via [Psico (PUCRS) DIF paper on the MAP](https://pucrs.emnuvens.com.br/revistapsico/article/view/27229). Volume/pages: PAID 47(5), 516–521 **[unverified]**.
- **Van Dam, Hobkirk, Danoff-Burg & Earleywine (2012)**, "Mind your words: Positive and negative items create method effects on the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire." They ran CFA on a large undergraduate (non-meditating) sample. A correlated-facets model fit better than a hierarchical (single higher-order mindfulness) model. **Negative and positive wording constituted substantive method effects.** The authors conclude that the FFMQ measures components that may relate to, but do not directly reflect, a latent mindfulness variable. They recommend against an FFMQ total score, favour facet scores, and call for further study of method effects in mindfulness scales. — [Penn State Pure record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/mind-your-words-positive-and-negative-items-create-method-effects/); [Mandala sources](https://sources.mandala.library.virginia.edu/node/109056). Journal *Assessment* 19(2), 198–204; sample size **[unverified]**.
- **Baer et al. (2008)**, *Assessment*, FFMQ in meditating and non-meditating samples. Most facets were related to meditation experience and to symptoms and well-being. Crucially, **the relationship between Observing and psychological adjustment varied with meditation experience**, so a facet's meaning depended on the sample. Several facets mediated the meditation → well-being relation. — [Contextual Science summary](https://contextualscience.org/publications/construct_validity_five_facet_mindfulness_questionnaire_meditating_nonmeditating); [author PDF (UKY)](https://tips.as.uky.edu/sites/default/files/2008%20Baer%20Five%20Facet%20Mindfulness%20Questionnaire.pdf). (Baer et al. 2006, *Assessment* 13(1), 27–45, is the original five-facet derivation from five pooled questionnaires. There, too, Observe loaded on the higher-order factor only in meditators **[unverified from retrieved text; widely reported]**.)
- **Gu et al. (2016)**, FFMQ-39 and FFMQ-15 before and after MBCT in adults with recurrent depression in remission. **Pre-MBCT**, a 4-factor hierarchical model *excluding Observing* fit best. **Post-MBCT**, a 5-factor hierarchical model fit best. **Configural invariance across time points was not supported** for either version. The authors note that pre/post comparisons may not be valid if the structure changes, and suggest excluding Observing from pre–post comparisons. — [Oxford ORA record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f55f8f8e-e686-41d1-89dd-d4651490c277); [Sussex figshare](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Examining_the_factor_structure_of_the_39-item_and_15-item_versions_of_the_five_facet_mindfulness_questionnaire_before_and_after_mindfulness-based_cognitive_therapy_for_people_with_recurrent_depression/23430056). Journal: *Psychological Assessment* 28(7), 791–802 **[unverified]**. Note that Gu et al. located the instability mainly in **Observe**, a positively keyed facet. Training changing an item's meaning is not confined to negative items.
- **Levin-Aspenson, Marks, Dalrymple & Zimmerman (2023)**, *J. Contextual Behavioral Science* 29, 131–139, doi:10.1016/j.jcbs.2023.07.001. This tested longitudinal invariance of the FFMQ-SF (24 items) from intake to discharge in **n = 2,928** psychiatric patients in an ACT-based partial hospital. The five-factor structure needed **correlated errors for similarly worded items** (Non-judging and Describe) to fit. Configural and weak invariance held for all scales. **Strong invariance held for Non-judging, Non-reactivity, Describe and Observing but NOT for Acting with Awareness.** Strict invariance was not supported. The authors frame violations as "response shift" and note the difficulty of "measuring constructs involving overt metacognitive processes via self-report." — [Contextual Science record](https://contextualscience.org/publications/levinaspenson_marks_dalrymple_zimmerman_2023)
- **Tran, Glück & Nader (2013)**, *J. Clinical Psychology* 69(9), 951–965. They built an FFMQ short form and found a **two-factor higher-order structure**: "Self-regulated Attention" and "Orientation to Experience". A 2014 follow-up (PLoS ONE) used multigroup CFA/ESEM with experienced meditators. — [Univ. Vienna record](https://klinische-gesundheit-psy.univie.ac.at/forschung/einzelansicht-publikationen/pure/9b09f08d-2235-4231-befd-cd642215d574/show/publ/Pure); [PMC4199716 (2014 paper)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4199716). Background, **[unverified]**: the higher-order split in this line of work tracks keying fairly closely. Acting with Awareness and Non-judging (negatively keyed) group together, versus Observe/Describe. That is why it has been read as partly a wording split.
- **Aguado et al. (2015)**, *Frontiers in Psychology* 6:404. Three pooled Spanish non-clinical samples, **n = 1,191**. A **bifactor** model (general mindfulness factor + 5 orthogonal facets) fit better than correlated five factors. **With the general factor partialled out, Acting with Awareness did not show adequate reliability.** Observing did not seem adequate for people without meditative experience. — [Frontiers full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00404/full); [UAB record](https://ddd.uab.cat/record/300285?ln=en)
- **Lilja et al. (Swedish FFMQ)**. N = 495 for the psychometric study and N = 817 (325 meditators, 317 non-meditators) for a person-oriented cluster analysis. Clusters in which meditators were over-represented all scored high on Observing, and clusters in which they were under-represented scored low on it. — [Springer, Mindfulness s12671-018-0990-4 / related record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12671-018-0990-4); [GUPEA thesis record](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/handle/2077/47520). I could not retrieve whether Lilja et al. modelled wording method factors. Treat that as a gap.
- **Takahashi et al. (2022)**, Japanese FFMQ short forms, N = 889. The 24-item FFMQ-SF fit adequately **only when uncorrelated method factors for positive and negative items were included** (CFI = 0.899, RMSEA = 0.059). The authors attribute the difference from earlier Japanese work to online sampling, where respondents may read instructions less carefully. Wording also affected correlations with external scales. — [Frontiers in Psychology 13:833381](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.833381/full)

### Inferences
- In the FFMQ, "negatively worded" and "Acting with Awareness / Non-judging" are nearly the same set of items. So a facet × group or facet × time finding cannot by itself separate a construct effect from a keying effect. The FFMQ is a cautionary design, not a model to copy.
- The DIF direction (meditators endorse positives more; non-meditators reject negatives more at equal trait) means training can plausibly move positive and negative items by different amounts. The difference reflects what respondents have learned to notice and how they read the item, not only the trait. For an RCT outcome, this is a time × arm interaction risk on the reverse-keyed items.
- Acting with Awareness is the reverse-keyed, "autopilot" facet, and it is the one that fails strong longitudinal invariance (Levin-Aspenson 2023). It also lacks specific reliability (Aguado 2015). That is directly relevant to a "drift-awareness of autopilot" facet in the Sense Foraging scale. Training *increases noticing* of autopilot, so people may endorse "I find myself on autopilot" *more* after training even as they spend less time on autopilot. That is the classic response-shift pattern (see Q2).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact sample sizes, fit statistics or the full list of DIF items for Van Dam 2009/2012.
- I did not retrieve Lilja et al.'s handling of method factors in meditators.
- I found no study that tests **wording-factor invariance across arms of a mindfulness RCT** (i.e., whether the negative-wording method factor itself changes with training). Gu 2016 and Levin-Aspenson 2023 test structural/longitudinal invariance, not a method factor's invariance.

## Q2. MAAS (all items negatively worded, scored as absence of mindlessness): critiques

### Takeaway
The MAAS measures mindfulness only by *reverse-scoring reports of mindlessness*. An IRT analysis found that only the general "autopilot" items were informative. It also found evidence of limited ability to report mindlessness and of response bias toward "mindfulness-absent" items. Grossman argued that people's self-assessed inattention is not a valid index of mindfulness and that training changes how people read such items.

### Cited Findings
- **Van Dam, Earleywine & Borders (2010)**, "Measuring mindfulness? An item response theory analysis of the Mindful Attention Awareness Scale," *Personality and Individual Differences*. IRT on **N = 414** undergraduates. General statements of **"automatic inattentiveness" / "automatic pilot"** gave the most statistical information about the latent trait. There was **evidence of limited abilities to report on mindlessness and of response bias to "mindfulness-absent" items**. The authors conclude that **reverse-scoring the scale may be inadequate to represent intentional attention or awareness**. — [Mandala sources record](https://sources.mandala.library.virginia.edu/node/109566). Vol/pages 49(7), 805–810 **[unverified]**. A later Rasch analysis identified the same highly discriminating item cluster. — [USQ record, Rasch analysis of MAAS](https://research.usq.edu.au/item/q340q/a-rasch-model-analysis-of-the-mindful-attention-awareness-scale)
- **Grossman (2011)**, *Psychological Assessment* 23(4), 1034–1040 **[citation details unverified]**, titled "Defining mindfulness by how poorly I think I pay attention during everyday awareness and other intractable problems for psychology's (re)invention of mindfulness." It questions whether self-report measures assess mindfulness at all, whether the construct can be understood apart from training, and whether there is evidence for the validity of the measures. **Brown, Ryan, Loverich, Biegel & West (2011)**, "Out of the armchair and into the streets," defended the MAAS in the same issue. — [Brown et al. 2011 PDF (SDT site)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2011_BrownEtAl_OutoftheArmchair.pdf); [Ewha record](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/out-of-the-armchair-and-into-the-streets-measuring-mindfulness-ad/). Grossman (2008, *J. Psychosomatic Research* 64, 405–408) **[unverified]** made the earlier argument that meditators and non-meditators may interpret the same items differently. Unpracticed respondents may lack the awareness needed to notice their own inattention, so low MAAS "mindlessness" can partly reflect *low insight* rather than high mindfulness.
- Example MAAS items: "I rush through activities without being really attentive to them"; "I find it difficult to stay focused on what's happening in the present". — via the search summary of the [Brown et al. 2011 PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2011_BrownEtAl_OutoftheArmchair.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "insight paradox" argued by Grossman and consistent with the Van Dam IRT bias finding applies directly to any Sense Foraging item that asks people to report *how often they drift onto autopilot*. Baseline reports in untrained people are likely under-reports. Training raises detection, so the reverse-keyed drift items could *worsen* (more drift reported) in the treatment arm while the construct improves. Consider wording drift-awareness items positively as **detection/catching** ("I notice when I have slipped into doing mode"). Do not use frequency-of-lapse items that are reverse-scored.
- The MAAS shows that an all-negative scale scores the absence of a deficit, not the presence of a skill. If reverse items are added, they should describe the presence of the *opposite* state, not merely the lack of the skill (see Q5).

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Grossman's (2008, 2011) exact text, so the "insight paradox" wording above is a paraphrase from background knowledge.
- I found no MAAS study that directly tests longitudinal invariance across an RCT.

## Q3. MAIA → MAIA-2: Not-Distracting and Not-Worrying

### Takeaway
In the original 32-item MAIA (2012), only Not-Distracting and Not-Worrying contained reverse-scored items, and both had poor internal consistency across many studies and translations. Mehling et al. (2018) did *not* rewrite them positively. They added five items (3 to Not-Distracting, all reverse-scored; 2 to Not-Worrying, one reverse-scored), which raised alphas only to **.74 and .67**. The authors called reverse scoring "a minor evil" accepted for theoretical reasons, and noted lower alphas in mind–body-inexperienced samples.

### Cited Findings
- **Mehling, Acree, Stewart, Silas & Jones (2018)**, "The Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness, Version 2 (MAIA-2)," *PLOS ONE* 13(12): e0208034. **N = 1,090** museum visitors (47% female, mean age 30.6). Rationale: Not-Distracting and Not-Worrying showed **"low internal consistency reliability"** across numerous studies. Two features were blamed. First, these were the only scales containing **reverse-scored "negative" items** (the others had only positive items). Second, each had **only three items**, and alpha is sensitive to item count. The authors acknowledged reverse scoring as "potentially problematic" but **"accept[ed] this as a minor evil"**, adding items rather than changing direction, to honour focus-group findings that distraction and worry are inverse dimensions of interoceptive awareness. **MAIA-2 alphas: Not-Distracting 0.74; Not-Worrying 0.67.** Alphas were **slightly lower in mind–body-inexperienced samples** than in trained practitioners. The MAIA-2 has 37 items. — [PLOS ONE full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0208034)
- New MAIA-2 items. Not-Distracting: "I try to ignore pain (R)"; "I push feelings of discomfort away by focusing on something else (R)"; "When I feel unpleasant body sensations, I occupy myself with something else so I don't have to feel them (R)". Not-Worrying: "I can stay calm and not worry when I have feelings of discomfort or pain" (positive); "When I am in discomfort or pain, I can't get it out of my mind (R)". — [MAIA-2 form, UCSF Osher Center](https://osher.ucsf.edu/sites/osher.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/MAIA-2.pdf); [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2022 validation](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.970982/full)
- Original MAIA (Mehling et al. 2012, *PLOS ONE* 7(11): e48230): the 2012 alphas were roughly **.66 (Not-Distracting) and .67 (Not-Worrying)** **[unverified; the retrieved MAIA-2 paper only says translation-study alphas were "below the published version and less than optimal"]**. — [2012 paper table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3486814/table/pone-0048230-t005)

### Inferences
- The MAIA's experience is the closest analogue to Sense Foraging: an interoception/contemplative scale in which the reverse-keyed subscales were the persistently unreliable ones. Even after a dedicated fix, Not-Worrying stayed below .70. The lower alphas in inexperienced samples imply a reliability × experience interaction. In an RCT, that could appear as differential reliability between arms at post-test.
- Note the content confound. The MAIA's reverse items are also the ones about *pain and discomfort* (avoidance, worry). The Sense Foraging facets "view under stress" and "normalizing stress narrowing" would carry the same confound if their reverse items are the only ones referring to aversive states.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve item-level loadings or the 2012 alphas from the primary text.
- I found no MAIA study that models a wording method factor explicitly or tests invariance pre/post training. Many MAIA intervention studies report subscale change, but I did not verify any that addressed keying.

## Q4. Other domain scales: TMS, PHLMS, CAMS-R, Self-Compassion Scale, awe, intolerance of uncertainty

### Takeaway
The Self-Compassion Scale is the best-documented case. Its negative items ("uncompassionate self-responding") behave so differently from its positive items that a two-factor reading was proposed. Neff et al. (2019) answered with a bifactor-ESEM model in which **95% of reliable variance** sat on a general factor. Critics (Muris & Otgaar) argue that the negative items mainly index psychopathology and inflate correlations with symptoms. For TMS, PHLMS, CAMS-R, awe and IU scales I found little or no published wording-effect work in this session. Their keying, as best I can tell, is listed below.

### Cited Findings
- **Neff et al. (2019)**, bifactor ESEM in **20 samples (N = 11,685)**: excellent fit for one general self-compassion factor plus six specific factors, with **95% of reliable variance attributable to the general factor**. Neff frames self-compassion as a system of compassionate self-responding *and reduced uncompassionate self-responding*. — [Neff, in-press manuscript](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Neff.inpress.pdf); [SCS information sheet](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/SCS-information.pdf); [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/133741). Journal: *Psychological Assessment* 31(1), 27–45 **[unverified]**.
- **Critique (Muris, Otgaar and colleagues)**: "Stripping the forest from the rotten trees: Compassionate self-responding is a way of coping, but reduced uncompassionate self-responding mainly reflects psychopathology." A meta-analysis of **16 studies** found a modest protective effect of positive self-compassion on anxiety/depression. It found a large and **significantly stronger** vulnerability effect of the negative subscales. The argument is that the negative items inflate SCS–psychopathology correlations and should be analysed separately. — [Neff 2018 letter (reply)](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Neff2018_Letter.pdf); [PMC9095813](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9095813/); ["On the edge of psychopathology: strong relations between reversed…" (BSL)](https://thim.mijn.bsl.nl/on-the-edge-of-psychopathology-strong-relations-between-reversed/26849034); [CIiPP 2023 two-factor SCS + intervention paper](https://www.termedia.pl/When-negativity-becomes-obstructive-a-novel-exploration-of-the-two-factor-model-of-the-Self-Compassion-Scale-and-a-comparison-of-self-compassion-and-self-criticism-interventions,75,42394,1,0.html)
- The SCS case is not a *pure* wording effect. Negative SCS items describe a *different behaviour* (self-judgement, isolation, over-identification), not a negation of the positive item. That is the "presence of the opposite" style (Q5), and it still produced a quasi-separate factor with distinct external correlates.
- **Toronto Mindfulness Scale (Lau et al. 2006)**: 13 items, Curiosity and Decentering, **all positively keyed** to my knowledge **[unverified; no source retrieved]**. It is a state measure designed for use right after a meditation session, and avoids the reversal problem by design.
- **CAMS-R (Feldman et al. 2007)**: 12 items with a small number of reverse-scored items (e.g., "I am easily distracted") **[unverified count and items]**. I found no published wording-effect analysis in this session.
- **Philadelphia Mindfulness Scale (Cardaciotto et al. 2008)**: 20 items, Awareness (positive) and Acceptance. The Acceptance items are worded as experiential avoidance/suppression and reverse-scored, so keying is **fully confounded with subscale**, as in FFMQ AwA/NJ. A revision of the PHLMS for measuring awareness exists. — [Illinois Experts record, "Revision of the Philadelphia Mindfulness Scale…"](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/revision-of-the-philadelphia-mindfulness-scale-for-measuring-awar/). The keying and item counts are **[unverified]**, and so is whether the revision addressed wording.
- **Intolerance of Uncertainty (IUS-27 / IUS-12)**: all items are keyed toward *intolerance* (no reverse items) **[unverified from retrieved text]**. Work on the IUS centres on factor structure (prospective/inhibitory) rather than keying. — [PMC6370219, "Seeking certainty about Intolerance of Uncertainty" (IUS-Revised)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6370219/). I could not confirm whether the IUS-R introduced balanced keying.
- **Awe scales** (e.g., the Awe Experience Scale, Yaden et al. 2019; dispositional awe in the DPES, Shiota et al. 2006): I found no wording-effect literature, and I did not verify their keying. Treat as a gap.
- Broader, non-domain evidence: reverse-keyed items typically affect factor structure and reliability, and there is no consensus on using them. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022, 13:828037 ("Examining the wording effect")](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.828037/full). One mindfulness factor-analytic study found a factor "mostly defined by negatively worded items," indicating wording artifacts across mindfulness questionnaires that "may threaten the stability of these facets". — [Karl et al., "Revisiting the five-facet structure of mindfulness" (MISS)](https://miss.psychopen.eu/index.php/miss/article/view/11015); [SSOAR record](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/handle/document/67963?show=full)

### Inferences
- Across the domain, the resolutions have been: (a) model it with a **bifactor** or method factor (SCS, FFMQ); (b) **add items** without rewriting direction (MAIA-2); (c) **avoid reversals entirely** (TMS, IUS); (d) **score positive and negative halves separately** (SCS critics, Van Dam's facet-only recommendation). None of these show that reversed items *improve* measurement in contemplative samples.
- The SCS debate warns about what will happen if Sense Foraging's reverse items describe *distress-laden opposites* (e.g., "When stressed, my world narrows and I can't see anything else"). They will probably correlate more with symptoms and neuroticism than the positive items do, and form a quasi-separate factor.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve primary sources on keying for the TMS, CAMS-R, PHLMS, awe scales or IUS-R. The keying statements above are background knowledge and need checking against the instruments.
- I found no published longitudinal invariance test of the SCS's positive vs negative halves across a self-compassion intervention, though such work may exist.

## Q5. Item-writing lessons in this domain

### Takeaway
Three recurring failure modes. (1) Items that score a skill by the *absence of a deficit* (MAAS, FFMQ AwA) depend on insight that untrained respondents lack. (2) "Non-X" constructs (non-judging, not-distracting, not-worrying) create double negatives when reverse-scored. (3) Reverse items in this domain tend to be about aversive content, which confounds keying with distress. Items written as the *presence of an opposite, observable behaviour* are preferable to negations, but even these form separate factors (SCS).

### Cited Findings
- Reverse-scoring descriptions of mindlessness "may be inadequate to represent intentional attention or awareness," and respondents show a limited ability to report mindlessness. — [Van Dam et al. 2010 (Mandala)](https://sources.mandala.library.virginia.edu/node/109566)
- Wording-based method effects in the FFMQ were strong enough that the authors recommended abandoning the total score. — [Van Dam et al. 2012 (Penn State)](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/mind-your-words-positive-and-negative-items-create-method-effects/)
- Inattentive online respondents are a candidate driver of method factors (needed in the Japanese FFMQ-SF only in an online sample). — [Takahashi et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.833381/full)
- MAIA-2 developers kept reverse items for theoretical fidelity and accepted lower reliability as "a minor evil". — [Mehling et al. 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0208034)
- Longitudinal invariance violations arise for constructs "involving overt metacognitive processes via self-report". — [Levin-Aspenson et al. 2023](https://contextualscience.org/publications/levinaspenson_marks_dalrymple_zimmerman_2023)
- Similarly worded items needed correlated residuals in the FFMQ-SF (Non-judging and Describe). — [Levin-Aspenson et al. 2023](https://contextualscience.org/publications/levinaspenson_marks_dalrymple_zimmerman_2023)

### Inferences (practical guidance for Sense Foraging)
- **Never negate a negated construct.** Facets named "non-judgement" or "not narrowing" invite items like "I don't avoid not…". Name the positive pole (completeness, allowing, widening) and, if reversing, use the *presence of the opposite behaviour* ("I label sensations as good or bad as soon as I notice them"), never "I do not…".
- **Avoid reverse items whose truth depends on awareness that training builds**, i.e., drift/autopilot frequency items. Training increases reported lapses (insight), so the reverse-scored change runs against the true change. Write drift-awareness as *catching* or *detecting*, positively keyed.
- **Avoid reverse items that are the only aversive-content items in a facet** (stress, discomfort). Otherwise keying is confounded with negative affect (the MAIA and SCS pattern). Balance aversive content across both keyings.
- **Spread reverse items across facets** rather than making whole facets reverse-keyed (the FFMQ AwA/NJ and PHLMS Acceptance trap). With ~8–10 reverse items over ~11 facets, put at most one per facet, so a wording factor can be modelled separately from facet factors.
- **Keep reading load low**: short, single-clause items. Online and attention-limited respondents generate method factors (Takahashi 2022), and the RCT will presumably be online.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve domain-specific studies on reading level or comprehension of mindfulness items (e.g., cognitive interviewing of FFMQ items). Some likely exist (cognitive-interview work on FFMQ/MAAS in clinical or older samples), but they were not found or verified here.

## Q6. Implications for measuring change in an RCT with mixed-keyed items

### Takeaway
The evidence predicts that a mixed-keyed contemplative scale will (a) need a wording method factor to fit, (b) risk non-invariance across time, especially for metacognitive/autopilot content and for facets whose meaning develops with practice, and (c) possibly show the treatment arm moving positive and negative items by different amounts. Testing longitudinal and between-arm invariance, with the wording factor modelled, should be pre-planned. Reverse-keyed items should be analysable as a separable set, so that the primary outcome can be shown to be robust to their inclusion.

### Cited Findings
- Violations of longitudinal measurement invariance indicate response shifts in how individuals understand and appraise items over time, and lead to misinterpretation of observed change. This is "particularly relevant" for mindfulness and process-based interventions. — [Levin-Aspenson et al. 2023 (via search summary)](https://contextualscience.org/publications/levinaspenson_marks_dalrymple_zimmerman_2023)
- Configural invariance of the FFMQ failed across MBCT. The structure itself changed (4 → 5 factors). — [Gu et al. 2016](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f55f8f8e-e686-41d1-89dd-d4651490c277)
- Strong (scalar) invariance failed for the reverse-keyed, autopilot facet (Acting with Awareness) over ACT treatment, n = 2,928. — [Levin-Aspenson et al. 2023](https://contextualscience.org/publications/levinaspenson_marks_dalrymple_zimmerman_2023)
- Meditation experience changes how keyed items are endorsed at equal trait level (18/39 DIF items). — [Van Dam et al. 2009](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1220506-differential-item-function-across-meditators-and-non-meditators-on-the-five-facet-mindfulness-questionnaire)
- Reliability of reverse-keyed subscales is lower in inexperienced samples. — [Mehling et al. 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0208034)

### Inferences
- Pre-register: (1) CFA with a positive/negative wording method factor at baseline; (2) longitudinal invariance (configural → metric → scalar) across pre/post, within arm; (3) multi-group invariance across arms at post-test; (4) a sensitivity analysis computing the primary outcome from positively keyed items only. If the effect holds on positive items alone and the wording factor is invariant, the reverse items are harmless. If not, the reverse items are carrying response shift.
- A 4-week course is short, but Gu 2016 found configural change over an 8-week MBCT. Expect some response shift even over 4 weeks, especially on drift-awareness, labelling and non-judgement content, which training explicitly teaches people to re-perceive.
- Plausible value of reverse items here: detecting acquiescent or careless responding, by comparing positive-minus-negative consistency, rather than adding construct coverage. If that is the goal, a handful of clearly worded "opposite-behaviour" items (fewer than 8–10) used as a response-quality check may give most of the benefit with less invariance risk. This is an inference, not a finding from the retrieved literature.

### Gaps
- I found no RCT in this domain that tests invariance of a *wording method factor* between arms, or that reports positively vs negatively keyed change scores separately. This is a real gap and could be a methodological contribution for the lab.
- Unverified whether later work (2020–2026) on FFMQ/MAIA response shift (e.g., then-test or retrospective-pretest designs in mindfulness trials) directly addresses keying. Not retrieved in this session.
