# Reverse-keyed items, acquiescence and careless responding: item-writing guidance

Context these notes were gathered for: a new 34-item agree-disagree (AD) scale, 6-point, fully labelled, no midpoint, per-item "Prefer not to answer", online (Prolific + students, many on phones), facets interleaved, two instructed-response attention checks already present. The lab plans to add about 8 to 10 reverse-keyed items.

Search notes: about 35 tool calls. Several primary sources were paywalled or behind a captcha (PMC HTML, Springer, Hogrefe, Vlerick repository). Full text was read for: Dykema et al. 2022 (via the NCBI BioC API), Clark & Watson 2019 (BioC), Boateng et al. 2018 (Europe PMC), Ilhan et al. 2024 (ERIC PDF), Curran & Hauser 2019 (PDF), Elek et al. 2025 (Frontiers), and Baumgartner's 2018 slide deck summarising Baumgartner, Weijters & Pieters 2018. For the other works only abstracts or index pages were available, and this is flagged where it matters. Anything I know from training but could not confirm in a source this session is listed under **Gaps** as "unverified" rather than cited.

## Q1. What do the major guides and the survey-methods literature say now? Is there a 2020–2026 consensus?

### Takeaway
No. Psychometric guides and studies have moved from "always balance the keying" to "reverse-key only with a clear reason, and prefer antonyms to negations". Survey methodologists go further and recommend item-specific (IS) questions over agree-disagree questions for most purposes. Nobody recommends adding negated items as they are commonly written. A 2025 review states outright that clear guidelines "cannot be provided" yet.

### Cited Findings
- **The survey-methods position (Dykema, Schaeffer, Garbarski, Assad, Blixt, 2022).** Title: "Towards a Reconsideration of the Use of Agree-Disagree Questions in Measuring Subjective Evaluations", *Research in Social and Administrative Pharmacy* (online 2021). The review identified **twenty** experimental AD-versus-IS comparisons. "More studies find IS questions are associated with desirable data quality outcomes (validity, reliability) and AD questions are associated with undesirable outcomes (acquiescence, response effects, etc.)". However, "a number of studies … find no differences … and a few studies find higher levels of undesirable outcomes for IS questions." On acquiescence specifically, "four studies reported no or inconsistent differences between AD and IS questions for acquiescence, [and] four studies reported AD questions were more susceptible". Conclusion: "we recommend IS questions over AD questions for most purposes." — [Dykema et al., author manuscript, PMC8692311](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311); [UWSC summary](https://uwsc.wisc.edu/lets-agree-to-disagree-new-publication-by-uwsc-researchers-recommends-against-using-agree-disagree-questions/)
- **Dykema et al.'s three objections to balancing an AD scale with reversals:**
  1. "Writing negatively worded questions that convey the same meaning across all respondents can be difficult." Their example: the opposite of "interesting" could be "not interesting", "uninteresting" or "boring", "but it is unlikely these have the same meaning across respondents". They add that reversals "will only reduce bias if respondents answer those items as extremely as they would their counterparts".
  2. "The use of negations like 'not,' 'un-,' 'non-,' and '-less' may decrease comprehensibility and data quality." This is "particularly problematic for AD items", where rejecting a negated statement requires processing a double negative.
  3. Balancing "may create methodological problems including lowering the validity and internal consistency of the measures and adding a method effect by creating an unexpected factor structure for the negatively-aligned items".

  — [Dykema et al. 2022, PMC8692311](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)
- **Dykema et al. also note an AD-specific acquiescence mechanism.** Acquiescence "can inflate estimates of mean scores, artificially inflate or deflate reliability estimates (particularly for items worded in the same direction), and create spuriously high correlations between AD measures and criterion measures". Acquiescence is "higher among respondents with lower education". — [Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)
- **Saris, Revilla, Krosnick & Shaeffer (2010)**, "Comparing questions with agree/disagree response options to questions with item-specific response options", *Survey Research Methods*, 4(1), 61–79. The design combined random-assignment split-ballot with multitrait-multimethod (MTMM) in representative samples. Responses to AD questions "had much lower quality than responses to comparable questions offering IS response options". — [SRM](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/srm/article/view/2682)
- **Weijters & Baumgartner (2012)**, "Misresponse to reversed and negated items in surveys: A review", *Journal of Marketing Research*. They reviewed 1,330 items from scales published in *JMR* and *JCR*. They list the advantages of reversed items as control of acquiescence, disruption of nonsubstantive responding, and better coverage of the construct's domain. The costs are low reliability, complex factor structures, and distorted relationships with other constructs. They **advocate continued use of reversed items, "with caution"**. — [PSU Pure record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/misresponse-to-reversed-and-negated-items-in-surveys-a-review/)
- **Suárez-Alvarez, Pedrosa, Lozano, García-Cueto, Cuesta & Muñiz (2018)**, "Using reversed items in Likert scales: A questionable practice", *Psicothema*, 30(2), 149–158. N = 374 adults, repeated-measures design with positive, reversed and mixed forms of a self-efficacy test. When positive and reversed items were combined, "the reliability of the test is flawed and the unidimensionality of the test is jeopardized by secondary sources of variance". — [Psicothema / Granada repository](https://digibug.ugr.es/handle/10481/52814); [PDF](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4463.pdf)
- **Ilhan, Güler, Teker & Ergenekon (2024)**, "The effects of reverse items on psychometric properties and respondents' scale scores according to different item reversal strategies", *International Journal of Assessment Tools in Education*, 11(1), 20–38, doi:10.21449/ijate.1345549. Their recommendation: "an instrument developer should be cautious about the usage of a mix of positive and reverse items … must make up his or her mind whether reverse items are really necessary and avoid using them unless there is a clear justification". — [ERIC full text](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440235.pdf)
- **Elek, Cígler, Grüning & Ježek (2025)**, "Advancing the psychometrics of reverse-keyed items: enriching cognitive theory by a logical and linguistic perspective", *Frontiers in Psychology*, 16, 1684612. The authors state: "No consensus has been achieved so far" and "as the linguistic aspects of wording effect has not been studied yet, a clear guidelines for scale development cannot be provided." Their estimate is that "up to 10% of systematic covariances across items in mixed-format scales may be related to reversals". — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)
- **Elek et al. (2025), citing Rečka et al. (2025):** the wording-factor problem "is significantly reduced" when an ordinal (non-linear) factor model is used instead of a linear one. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)
- **Clark & Watson (2019)**, "Constructing validity: New developments in creating objective measuring instruments", *Psychological Assessment*, 31(12), 1412–1427, doi:10.1037/pas0000626. A full-text search of the manuscript found **no specific recommendation on reverse-keying or balancing for acquiescence**. Its item-writing emphasis is on "paying careful attention to item wording", avoiding double-barrelled items (example: "I would never drink and drive for fear that I might be stopped by the police"), and "emphasizing unidimensionality over internal consistency". — [Europe PMC record PMC6754793](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30896212)
- **Boateng et al. (2018)**, "Best Practices for Developing and Validating Scales for Health, Social, and Behavioral Research: A Primer", *Frontiers in Public Health*, 6, 149. A full-text search found **no guidance on reverse-keyed or negatively worded items**. It advises only that "items should be worded simply and unambiguously" and that pre-testing should be used to catch poorly worded items. — [Frontiers / PMC6004510](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2018.00149)

### Inferences
- The two traditions point the same way. Psychometricians say "few, well-made reversals, antonym-based, and model them". Survey methodologists say "avoid AD where you can". For this lab, keeping AD format (for comparability across the 34 items) is defensible, but it should be written down as a deliberate choice.
- With 8–10 reversals out of roughly 42–44 items, the scale is partially balanced (about 20–23% reversed). That is enough to estimate an acquiescence factor and to create antonym pairs. It is not enough to cancel acquiescence in raw sum scores, which needs full balance (see Q4). The analysis plan should therefore model acquiescence or wording rather than rely on the sum score to correct it.
- Because reversals can create a separate method factor, preregister the model in advance: either a random-intercept (acquiescence) factor or a bifactor/wording factor, estimated with ordinal (WLSMV) estimation. Do not let a two-factor "positive/negative" EFA solution be read as substantive.

Confirm in Chapter 5 ("Guidelines in scale development") before citing.
- **Streiner, Norman & Cairney (2015, *Health Measurement Scales*, 5th ed.), Furr (2021/2022, *Psychometrics* / *Scale Construction and Psychometrics for Social and Personality Psychology*)**: not accessed. Unverified recollection: Streiner et al. discuss acquiescence and caution that negatively worded items lower reliability, especially in some populations.

## Q2. How to write good reversed items

### Takeaway
Reverse the **construct**, not the grammar. Use a canonical polar opposite (antonym) of the same core concept, at the same intensity, with the same length and reading level. Avoid "not / un- / never" negations, because rejecting them requires a double negative in AD format. Write each reversal as the semantic partner of a specific forward item in the same facet, and check that the opposite is an opposite *on the construct's continuum*, not a neighbouring construct.

### Cited Findings
- **The 2×2 taxonomy (Baumgartner, Weijters & Pieters 2018; Elek et al. 2025).**
  - Regular item: "Talkative" / "I am tall"
  - Negated regular: "Not talkative" — reversed by negation
  - Polar opposite: "Quiet" — reversed by antonym
  - Negated polar opposite: "Not quiet" — not reversed, but contains both a negation and an antonym

  Each type invites its own misresponse:
  - *Negation MR*: missing the "not"
  - *Polar-opposite MR*: not treating "quiet" as the opposite of "talkative"
  - *Reversal MR*: responding to every reversed item as if it were regular

  In the illustrative extraversion data, standardised loading shortfalls relative to reference items were:

  | Misresponse type | Estimate | 95% CI |
  |---|---|---|
  | Negation MR | −.17 | [−.58, .24] |
  | Polar-opposite MR | −.55 | [−.97, −.15] |
  | Reversal MR | −.26 | [−.68, .15] |

  Only the polar-opposite effect excluded zero in that example.

  — [Baumgartner (2018), slides summarising Baumgartner, Weijters & Pieters 2018, *Journal of Business Research*?](https://wiwi.univie.ac.at/fileadmin/user_upload/f_wiwi/News/Events/2018/Baumgartner.pdf); [Tilburg record of "Misresponse to survey questions: a conceptual framework…"](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/misresponse-to-survey-questions-a-conceptual-framework-and-empiri/). Journal name unverified; see Gaps.
- **Eye tracking: inattention versus difficulty.** Baumgartner et al. separate two mechanisms. Misresponse from *inattention* shows no increase in gaze duration on the problem item. Misresponse from *difficulty* happens "despite more extensive processing". — [Baumgartner 2018 slides](https://wiwi.univie.ac.at/fileadmin/user_upload/f_wiwi/News/Events/2018/Baumgartner.pdf); [PSU Pure record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/misresponse-to-survey-questions-a-conceptual-framework-and-empiri/)
- **Antonym vs negation, experimental.** Ilhan et al. (2024): N = 1,166 Turkish high-school students, 10-item school-attitude scale in three forms. Form-P was all positive. Form-RA had 5 positive + 5 antonym reversals. Form-RN had 5 positive + 5 negation reversals.
  - Form-P was unidimensional. Both mixed forms were two-dimensional.
  - McDonald's ω: Form-P .861 [.849, .874]; Form-RA .855 [.842, .868]; Form-RN .816 [.800, .832].
  - Correlation with Form-P: Form-RA r = .695, Form-RN r = .647.
  - Form-RN means differed significantly (small effect).
  - Form-RA had higher loadings and higher concurrent validity.
  - Conclusion: "prefer items created with antonyms instead of items built with negations".

  The items were built so that reversals kept "the meaning and wording severity of the regular items". — [Ilhan et al. 2024](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440235.pdf)
- **Conflicting evidence.** Ilhan et al. note that their antonym result contrasts with Suárez-Alvarez et al. (2018). In the latter, the mixed form *with antonyms* explained less variance than the all-positive form. Ilhan et al. attribute the difference to language, sample, or the instrument. — [Ilhan et al. 2024](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440235.pdf); [Suárez-Alvarez et al. 2018](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4463.pdf)
- **Why negations fail.** Ilhan et al. cite Zhang et al. (2016): a negated item "might engender careless responding … misread I am not happy as I am happy", and disagreeing with a negation requires extra mental steps. — [Ilhan et al. 2024](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440235.pdf)
- **Antonym choice matters: canonicity, contraries vs contradictories (Elek et al. 2025).**
  - "With decreasing canonicity, disagreement among people increases about which antonyms are real opposites." When using "any but a canonical pair of antonyms (e.g., good-bad), the possibility increases substantially that respondents do not understand the two oppositely keyed items as true opposites."
  - *Contradictories* are bounded, mutually exclusive pairs (alive/dead). *Contraries* are unbounded pairs (tall/short) where it is logical to disagree with both. Their example: an average-height person should disagree with both "I am tall" and "I am short". That "inconsistency" is valid, not error.
  - Negations shift toward an antonym asymmetrically: "I am not big" tends to be read as "I am small".

  — [Elek et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)
- **Same-meaning problem (Dykema et al. 2022).** "Not interesting", "uninteresting" and "boring" are unlikely to mean the same thing across respondents. Reversals only reduce bias "if respondents answer those items as extremely as they would their counterparts". This argues for matching intensity. — [PMC8692311](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)
- **Ilhan et al.'s worked example of the same distinction:** "The conditions of my life are not good" (negation) vs "The conditions of my life are bad" (antonym). They also note the Ilhan/Zhang example that "relaxed" may not be read as the opposite of "anxious". — [Ilhan et al. 2024](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440235.pdf)
- **A published scale that shows the "different construct" trap.** The Material Values Scale (Richins & Dawson 1992), as shown by Baumgartner:
  - Regular: "I'd be happier if I could afford to buy more things"
  - Reversed: "I have all the things I really need to enjoy life"
  - Reversed: "I wouldn't be any happier if I owned nicer things" (a negation)

  The first reversal measures contentment or sufficiency, not low materialism. — [Baumgartner 2018 slides](https://wiwi.univie.ac.at/fileadmin/user_upload/f_wiwi/News/Events/2018/Baumgartner.pdf)
- **Avoid double-barrelled items** that assess a behaviour and a motive at once. — [Clark & Watson 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30896212). Curran & Hauser (2019) found double-barrelling produces thoughtful "wrong" answers, as in "I'm paid biweekly, just not by leprechauns". — [Curran & Hauser 2019, *J. Research in Personality*, 82, 103849](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/2019-curran.pdf)

### Inferences (practical rules for drafting the 8–10 items)
1. **Pick the concept first, then the word.** For each reversal, name the low pole of the facet's continuum: what a person *low* on the facet experiences or does. Do not just negate a forward item. If the low pole is "absence" (e.g., "I rarely notice X"), use a frequency or extent antonym such as "rarely" or "seldom". Do not use "difficulty" ("I find it hard to notice X"). Difficulty brings in ability, effort and distress, which is a different construct. This matches Elek et al.'s warning about non-canonical opposites.
2. **No "not", "never", "un-", "-less", "don't" in the stem.** In a 6-point AD format with no midpoint, disagreeing with a negated stem is a double negative (Dykema et al.; Ilhan et al.). Exception: a lexicalised antonym that respondents read as one word (e.g., "unaware" may be acceptable where "aware" is canonical). Test these in cognitive interviews.
3. **Use canonical, bounded-feeling opposites** (notice/miss, often/rarely, easy/hard only if ease is the construct). Avoid contraries that leave a sensible middle. Without a midpoint, respondents in that middle are forced to pick a side, and both items then look inconsistent.
4. **Match intensity, length, reading level and grammar** to the paired forward item. Ilhan et al. built theirs "without changing the meaning and wording severity". A mild forward item paired with an extreme reversal will not mirror it, and the antonym pair will under-correlate.
5. **Pair each reversal to one forward item in the same facet,** with the same context or referent ("in the last week", "when I'm stressed"). This gives psychometric-antonym pairs for screening (Q3) and keeps facet content balanced.
6. **Write about twice as many as needed and pilot,** in line with the general item-pool advice in [Boateng et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2018.00149). Drop reversals whose pilot correlation with their paired forward item is weak. Do cognitive interviews on phones specifically to catch missed negations.

### Gaps
- The exact journal for Baumgartner, Weijters & Pieters (2018), "Misresponse to survey questions: A conceptual framework and empirical test of the effects of reversals, negations, and polar opposite core concepts", is unverified. I believe it is *Journal of Marketing Research*? or *Journal of Business Research*?, but could not confirm. Check the PSU or Tilburg record before citing.
- I found no study that directly tested "absence vs difficulty" framings of a reversed item. That rule is an inference from construct-validity principles and Elek et al.'s canonicity argument.

## Q3. Using reversed pairs to detect careless responding vs instructed-response items; Prolific/MTurk evidence

### Takeaway
Antonym pairs are a legitimate, cheap second screen. Curran's (2016) convention is to pair items whose sample correlation is ≤ −.60, and they have good specificity and modest sensitivity. They work only if the pairs are true opposites and the scale has enough of them. Instructed-response items are the most transparent screen, but they are not error-free. Current guidance is to combine several indicators rather than rely on any single one. Prolific yields higher attention-check pass rates than MTurk, Qualtrics panels or student (SONA) pools.

### Cited Findings
- **Ward & Meade (2023)**, "Dealing with careless responding in survey data: Prevention, identification, and recommended best practices", *Annual Review of Psychology*, 74, 577–596. Definition: careless responding "occurs when participants are not basing their response on the item content … when a respondent does not read an item, does not understand an item, or is unmotivated". The review covers prevention, identification and cleaning. — [SSRN record](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4337272); [summary of definition via arXiv paper citing it](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.07167)
- **Meade & Craig (2012)** grouped detection methods as: (a) special items (bogus or instructed), (b) consistency indices (psychometric synonyms/antonyms, even-odd), (c) multivariate outliers, (d) response time, and (e) self-reported diligence. Roughly **12% of undergraduates** engaged in egregious careless responding. Rates across studies run "from the low single digits to upwards of 30% or 40%". — [secondary summaries via search; Meade & Craig 2012, *Psychological Methods*, 17(3), 437–455](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1368390/pdf). Numbers were taken from secondary summaries, not the original; verify.
- **Psychometric antonyms: the cutoff convention.** Curran's procedure pairs items whose sample correlation is "−.60 or lower" for antonyms or ".60 or higher" for synonyms. Each respondent's within-person correlation across those pairs is then the index. — [Curran 2016 as described in the retrieved document; the R `careless` package implements `psychant()`/`psychsyn()` with a critical value parameter](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/packages/careless/index.html). Exact reference: Curran, P. G. (2016). Methods for the detection of carelessly invalid responses in survey data. *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology*, 66, 4–19. The citation details are from my knowledge; the search tool returned a garbled version.
- **Performance of antonyms.** A comparative study recommended psychometric antonyms, response time and individual reliability "as candidates for future application … due to their high specificity and moderate sensitivity". — [SAJIP article and search summary; possibly Niessen, Meijer & Tendeiro 2016, *J. Research in Personality*, "Detecting careless respondents in web-based questionnaires: Which method to use?"](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/detecting-careless-respondents-in-web-based-questionnaires-which-/). The attribution should be verified.
- **Instructed-response items have false positives (Curran & Hauser 2019),** "I'm paid biweekly, just not by leprechauns: Evaluating valid-but-incorrect response rates to attention check items", *J. Research in Personality*, 82, 103849. Design: verbal protocols, N = 59 + 30 students.
  - Thoughtful participants often gave justified "wrong" answers to bogus items (e.g., agreeing they "can teleport" because walking is a kind of teleportation).
  - For instructed-response items ("Answer with 'Agree' for this item"), "each identified at least one individual as careless, despite these individuals reading and responding to 135 other items in a thoughtful way".
  - "Simple known truths" items had the lowest false-positive rates.
  - Worked example of the cost: an item with a 5% false-positive rate means the sample must be 5% larger, or is 5% smaller after cleaning.

  — [Curran & Hauser 2019 PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/2019-curran.pdf)
- **Instructed-manipulation-check failure rates:** "Between 14% and 46% of respondents failed this test in Oppenheimer et al. (2009)". — [Baumgartner 2018 slides](https://wiwi.univie.ac.at/fileadmin/user_upload/f_wiwi/News/Events/2018/Baumgartner.pdf)
- **Platform comparison (Douglas, Ewell & Brauer 2023)**, "Data quality in online human-subjects research: Comparisons between MTurk, Prolific, CloudResearch, Qualtrics, and SONA", *PLOS ONE*, 18(3), e0279720. Prolific and CloudResearch participants "were more likely to pass various attention checks, provide meaningful answers, follow instructions, remember previously presented information, have a unique IP address and geolocation, and work slowly enough to be able to read all the items" than MTurk, Qualtrics or the undergraduate SONA sample. Cost per high-quality respondent: Prolific $1.90, CloudResearch $2.00, MTurk $4.36, Qualtrics $8.17. SONA was free but slowest. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0279720.html); [Prolific citations page](https://citations.prolific.com/paper/data-quality-in-online-human-subjects-research-comparisons-between-mturk-prolific-cloudresearch-qual)
- **Careless responders create the "reversed-item factor" (Kam & Meyer 2015)**, "How careless responding and acquiescence response bias can influence construct dimensionality: The case of job satisfaction", *Organizational Research Methods*, 18(3), 512–541. Careless responding and acquiescence can both produce spurious dimensionality in mixed-keyed scales. — [search record](https://hrcak.srce.hr/261661) (detail from abstract-level information only)

### Inferences
- **Recommended screening stack for this scale:**
  1. Keep the two instructed-response items, phrased as "simple known truths" or direct instructions, not bogus whimsical claims. These catch gross inattention.
  2. Build 4–5 a priori antonym pairs from the new reversals and confirm the r ≤ −.60 criterion in pilot data. Compute a within-person antonym correlation, but use it as a flag in combination with other indicators, not as a sole exclusion rule. Five pairs give a noisy within-person r.
  3. Add response time per page (from the platform) and longstring.
  4. Preregister the exclusion rule.
- **Combining indicators is the safer course.** Curran & Hauser's false-positive data and Elek et al.'s "contraries" argument both show that a single "inconsistent" answer can come from a thoughtful respondent. Pairs built from contraries (where both can be legitimately disagreed with) will flag mid-trait respondents. With no midpoint on the 6-point scale this risk is higher.
- **Antonym screening needs true reversals.** It works only if the reversed items mirror forward items in content. That is another reason to write reversals as direct semantic partners (Q2).

### Gaps
- **Ward & Meade (2023) specific recommendations** (e.g., suggested number of attention checks, "multiple hurdles" approach): the full text was not retrieved. Unverified recollection: they recommend prevention (warnings, reasonable length), several detection methods, and transparent reporting of exclusions.
- **Peer, Rothschild, Gordon, Evernden & Damer (2022)**, "Data quality of platforms and panels for online behavioral research", *Behavior Research Methods*, 54, 1643–1662: not retrieved. Unverified recollection: Prolific and CloudResearch outperformed MTurk, Qualtrics and Dynata on attention, comprehension and honesty.
- No direct head-to-head sensitivity/specificity comparison of antonym pairs vs instructed items in a Prolific sample was found.
- **DeSimone, Harms & DeSimone (2015)**, "Best practice recommendations for data screening", *Journal of Organizational Behavior*: not retrieved.

## Q4. Alternatives to reverse-keyed items for acquiescence

### Takeaway
There are four options, each with costs:
1. **Item-specific response formats**: best evidence on quality, but breaks the uniform AD grid.
2. **Forced-choice**: removes acquiescence but yields ipsative data that complicates normative scoring.
3. **A separate balanced acquiescence marker scale**: lets you estimate acquiescence without touching the target scale, but adds items.
4. **Statistical modelling only** (random-intercept or bifactor factor): requires some balance to identify.

Partial balancing plus modelling is a reasonable middle course.

### Cited Findings
- **Item-specific questions** directly ask about the underlying dimension. Example: "How satisfied are you: not at all…extremely" instead of "I am extremely satisfied: strongly agree…strongly disagree".
  - Benefits: higher reliability and validity in most comparisons ([Saris et al. 2010](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/srm/article/view/2682); [Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)). Unipolar IS items "offer more points of differentiation" and "may have lower correlated method variance". GSS unipolar questions "were more reliable than bipolar questions".
  - Costs: converting AD items to IS "often reveals that the items do not share the same underlying response dimension" (intensity vs quantity vs frequency). The grid then needs several response sets, and IS items took longer to answer.

  — [Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311)
- **Device evidence on IS vs AD.** A split-ballot in the Netquest panel (Spain, N = 1,476) compared AD and IS questions on PCs and smartphones. IS questions had longer response times and "better response quality" than AD on both devices. — [Hogrefe *Methodology* article, likely Höhne, Revilla & Lenzner 2018](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1614-2241/a000151) (abstract-level via search summary; author attribution unverified)
- **Partial balance alone does not cancel acquiescence in means.** The logic of balancing "will reduce bias in scale means by placing those who acquiesce in the middle of the response distribution", which requires equal numbers of forward and reversed items with equal extremity. — [Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311). Likert (1932) recommended that "half of the scale should refer to the opposite pole" — [Elek et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)
- **Ordinal modelling** substantially reduces the reversed-item method problem compared with linear factor analysis (Rečka et al. 2025, cited in Elek et al. 2025). — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)
- **Balanced-scale approaches** to acquiescent response style are discussed in a University of Michigan deposit, "Using Balanced Scales to Address Acquiescent Response Style". — [Deep Blue](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/53f98f78-ec4b-47d9-8dc8-69efe66bbb49/full). Content not read; listed only as a lead.

### Inferences
- **Practical recommendation:** keep AD for the 34 items, add the 8–10 antonym reversals, and estimate acquiescence with a random-intercept factor model in which all items load 1 on an acquiescence factor (Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman 2006 style; unverified citation). The reversals are what identify that factor. If budget allows, a short balanced marker set of unrelated content (6–10 items) would let acquiescence be estimated independently of the construct. That is the strongest design but adds length.
- **IS conversion** is the evidence-favoured option for a future version. The cost here is mixed response sets on a phone screen and breaking a 6-point fully labelled grid that is already settled for the lab.
- **Forced-choice** is a poor fit for a facet scale meant for normative comparison and for a first validation.

### Gaps
- **Forced-choice evidence** (e.g., Thurstonian IRT, Brown & Maydeu-Olivares 2011) was not retrieved this session.
- **The random-intercept acquiescence model** (Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman 2006, *Psychological Methods*; Billiet & McClendon 2000) was not retrieved; citations unverified.
- **Separate acquiescence marker scales** (e.g., Weijters, Baumgartner and colleagues' heterogeneous-item ARS indices): no source retrieved.

## Q5. Placement: dispersed vs grouped; how many is enough

### Takeaway
Disperse reversed items through the questionnaire, keeping each reversal **away** from its forward partner. In Weijters, Geuens & Schillewaert (2009), N = 3,114, reversed-pair correlations became stronger (more negative) as the distance between the two items increased. Nearby reversed items push respondents to retrieve "new and different information", which weakens the expected negative correlation. On proportion, there is no evidence-based minimum. Full balance (50%) is the classical ideal for cancelling acquiescence in sums. Recent guidance prefers few, well-made reversals plus modelling.

### Cited Findings
- **Weijters, Geuens & Schillewaert (2009)**, "The proximity effect: The role of inter-item distance on reverse-item bias", *International Journal of Research in Marketing*, 26(1), 2–12. Cognitive interviews plus N = 3,114 survey data.
  - "The (positive) correlation between a nonreversed item pair decreases with increasing inter-item distance, while the (negative) correlation between reversed item pairs decreases (i.e., becomes stronger) with increasing inter-item distance."
  - Mechanism: respondents "minimize retrieval of additional information when answering nearby nonreversed items" and "maximize retrieval of new and different information when answering nearby reversed items".
  - Recommendation: "the use of reversed items dispersed throughout the questionnaire".

  — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ijrema/v26y2009i1p2-12.html); [Vlerick repository](https://repository.vlerick.com/entities/publication/1779247d-61d9-4016-8e43-f96693eec31f)
- **A partially contrary 2021/2022 finding:** "Proximity of Items Does Not Necessarily Affect Their Correlation" (*European Journal of Psychological Assessment*, Hogrefe, doi:10.1027/1015-5759/a000661). The title suggests the proximity effect did not replicate in all conditions. Only the title was seen. — [Hogrefe](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1015-5759/a000661)
- **A 2025 preprint, "Survey item placement has little effect on data quality or treatment-effect estimates"**, is another null-leaning placement result (political science, not reversed items specifically). — [APSA preprint](https://preprints.apsanet.org/engage/apsa/article-details/6a7486f7d1922e37d520c365)
- **"Separating truth from error: A closer look at the effect of grouped versus intermixed questionnaire format"** (CEIBS repository) is a relevant lead on grouped vs intermixed items. Not read. — [CEIBS](https://repository.ceibs.edu/en/publications/separating-truth-from-error-a-closer-look-at-the-effect-of-groupe)
- **Ordering advice from the 2022–2024 review literature:** open a mixed scale with the highest-loading positive item, then the highest-loading reversed item. — [Frontiers search summary (Frontiers in Psychology 2024 article on reverse items)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1494261/full). Exact article and authors not confirmed.
- **Proportion:** Likert (1932) recommended half. The Elek et al. (2025) review declines to recommend a specific proportion. — [Elek et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1684612/full)

### Inferences
- Because facets are already interleaved, spread the 8–10 reversals so that each sits at least several screens or items away from its paired forward item. Avoid two reversals in a row. Avoid placing a reversal immediately after an attention check, because that confounds the two screens.
- Do not put a reversal as the very first item. Open with clear, high-loading forward items to establish the response frame.
- At about 20% reversed, the scale is unbalanced for raw-score acquiescence correction. Either accept that and model acquiescence (Q4), or distribute the reversals evenly across facets (about 1–2 per facet) so that no facet's subscore is disproportionately affected.

### Gaps
- **Schriesheim and colleagues' item-ordering studies** (e.g., Schriesheim, Kopelman & Solomon 1989; Schriesheim & Hill 1981 on negated items reducing accuracy): not retrieved. Unverified recollection: Schriesheim & Hill (1981) found that mixed-wording conditions reduced response accuracy compared with all-regular wording.
- **No empirical minimum number of reversals** for identifying an acquiescence factor was found. Unverified: the random-intercept model is typically identifiable with partial balance (e.g., 2+ reversed items) if the items are on a common scale.
- The null-replication paper on proximity (Hogrefe 2021/22) was not read. Its sample, conditions and conclusion are unknown.

## Q6. Response-format and device interactions

### Takeaway
Scale format strongly affects misresponse to reversed items. Weijters, Cabooter & Schillewaert (2010), N = 1,207 + 226, varied the number of categories and whether all categories or only the endpoints were labelled. They found "strong effects of scale format on response distributions and misresponse to reversed items". I could not retrieve the direction of each effect. On devices, recent evidence shows only small, non-systematic smartphone vs PC differences in acquiescence and satisficing, so phone delivery alone is not a strong reason to change the reversal plan.

### Cited Findings
- **Weijters, Cabooter & Schillewaert (2010)**, "The effect of rating scale format on response styles: The number of response categories and response category labels", *International Journal of Research in Marketing*, 27(3), 236–247, doi:10.1016/j.ijresmar.2010.02.004. An online survey (N = 1,207) had 8 conditions crossing number of categories with full vs endpoint labelling; a follow-up had N = 226. "We find evidence of strong effects of scale format on response distributions and misresponse to reversed items and formulate recommendations on scale format choice." — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ijrema/v27y2010i3p236-247.html); [HAL record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-00787535.html)
- **AD middle categories:** "Studies indicate respondents use the middle category when answering AD questions in unwanted ways." — [Dykema et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692311). This is relevant support for the lab's no-midpoint design. With no midpoint, however, contrary-type antonym pairs force mid-trait respondents into apparent inconsistency (Elek et al. 2025).
- **Devices:** A study of nationally representative ISSP samples (2018, 2019) used seven response-quality indicators. It found "only small, nonsystematic differences between devices on satisficing indicators such as acquiescence, non-substantive answers, selection of mid-point response options and primacy effects, and straightlining". — [SSOAR / Sage, 10.1177/00491241221077237](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00491241221077237). A crossover experiment in a probability web panel found response quality "equivalent between mobile and PC Web". — [search summary; see surveyinsights.org device-effects PDF](https://surveyinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/device-effects-on-survey-response-quality.pdf)
- **IS vs AD on PCs and smartphones (Netquest, N = 1,476):** IS was better than AD in quality on both devices, at the cost of longer response times. — [Hogrefe *Methodology*](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1614-2241/a000151)

### Inferences
- On phones, the realistic reversal risk is **inattention-type misresponse**: skimming past the key word. A negation ("not") is a single short token, easily missed on a small screen, which strengthens the antonym-over-negation rule. This is an inference; no phone-specific negation study was found.
- With full labelling, each label is visible. Keep labels short so the 6 options fit without horizontal scroll on narrow screens. Use the same direction (Strongly disagree → Strongly agree) on every item so that reversals are reversed in content only, never in layout.

### Gaps
- **Direction of the Weijters et al. (2010) effects is not verified.** Unverified recollection: full labelling increased net acquiescence relative to endpoint-only labelling, and misresponse to reversed items was lower with more categories and/or full labelling. Their recommendation may have favoured 7-point endpoint-labelled formats for some purposes and fully labelled 5-point for others. **Do not cite a direction without reading the paper** (IJRM 27(3), 236–247).
- **No study found specifically on 6-point no-midpoint formats and reversed-item misresponse.**
- **No phone-specific experiment found on negated vs antonym reversed items.**
- **Höhne & Lenzner smartphone/eye-tracking papers** on AD vs IS: not retrieved directly. The Netquest study is plausibly Höhne, Revilla & Lenzner (2018), *Methodology*, 14(3); unverified.
