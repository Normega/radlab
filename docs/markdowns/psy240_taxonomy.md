## 1. Course frame, as taught

| | |
|---|---|
| Structure | **11 × 3-hour lectures** (not 12–20 shorter classes), ~2 textbook chapters each |
| Textbook | **Barlow & Durand, *Abnormal Psychology*, Canadian edition** (Cengage/Nelson, MindTap) — **this is the dependency the wiki exists to remove** |
| Chapters used | Barlow ch. 1–17, all of them, in a non-linear order (personality ch. 13 is taught last, after ch. 14–16) |
| Assessment 2025 | Weekly textbook quizzes 10%, weekly textbook case studies 10%, participation 5%, RCT participation + report 5%, **Case Study Report 10%**, midterm 25%, final 35% |
| Exams | Midterm online via Quercus (50 MC + 10 definition matching + 2-of-4 short answer). Final in person, 40 MC + 3-of-4 short answer, ~70–80% new material |
| Classroom app | `emotion-fall2025.web.app` — Mood Dashboard + Ask Questions + "Emotion Form". Every lecture opens with a Mood Dashboard beat and an Emotion Form prompt, plus 2–3 mid-lecture break beats. **This is what Lecture Lounge replaces** (see Appendix A for the full prompt library) |
| In-class RCT | Students are participants: baseline / mid / post surveys to `@mail.utoronto.ca`, daily check-ins, debrief lecture, report assignment. Conditions: **Self Care vs. Sense Foraging** training; DVs: pos/neg affect, stress, life satisfaction, depression/anxiety, body awareness, decentering. (Note the utoronto-email dependency — relevant to WP5's roster match key.) |
| Recurring motifs | Stress-diathesis as the through-line; brain networks (salience network, "3 Big Networks"); Norm's own lab work (MBCT/WB-CT trial, PMID 35367955; Air Transat 236 traumatic-memory fMRI); explicit multiple-choice strategy coaching; grounding/self-care closers |

### Two facts that change the plan

1. **11 lectures, not 20 classes.** The plan's shape — "20 class overviews + 2–5 anchor
   disorders each" — was sized against a guessed class count. The real teaching unit is the
   3-hour lecture covering ~2 chapters, so a lecture page is *navigation*, and the natural
   anchor for content is the **DSM-5-TR diagnostic class** (§4). Recount in §6.
2. **The course teaches ~100 distinct diagnoses.** Complete coverage of what is actually
   named in the decks does not fit a 15-hour review pass. §5 resolves this with a two-tier
   split rather than by silently dropping material — see §6 for what that costs.

## 3. What is *not* in this course

Recorded so the taxonomy's omissions are deliberate rather than accidental: **elimination
disorders** (enuresis/encopresis), **medication-induced movement disorders**, and DSM's
**"other specified / unspecified"** residual categories are never named in any deck. Selective
mutism, tic disorders, and communication disorders get one line each. Do not scaffold pages
for the first three at all; the second three are Tier B at most.

## 4. DSM-5-TR chapter DOI map — **resolved 2026-07-25** (closes open question #7)

**All 18 taught chapters are verified.** No myaccess session was needed: PsychiatryOnline 403s
automated fetches, but its chapter pages are search-indexed, so each chapter's real DOI was
recovered from the index by exact-phrase search on the DOI stem (`"appi.books.9780890425787.x03"`
→ the indexed "Bipolar and Related Disorders | Psychiatry Online" result carries the full slug in
its URL). Store the result as `disorders.dsm_chapter_doi` — or better, a `dsm_chapters` lookup,
since it is per-chapter, not per-disorder.

**The prediction was half right, and the wrong half would have shipped broken links.** The
*ordinal* hypothesis held perfectly — chapters 1–19 are `x01`–`x19` in manual order. The
*name* half did not: 5 of the 19 slugs are not the chapter title slugified, and there is no rule
that generates them.

- `x02_Schizophrenia_Spectrum` — truncated, drops "and Other Psychotic Disorders"
- `x15_Disruptive_Impulse_Control` — truncated, drops "and Conduct Disorders"
- `x16_Substance_Related_Disorders` — truncated, drops "and Addictive"
- `x12_Sleep-Wake_Disorders` — keeps the **hyphen** where every other slug converts punctuation
  to `_`
- `x14_Gender_Dysophoria` — **misspelled in the official DOI** ("Dysophoria"). Not a
  transcription error on our side; that is the live URL.

So a generated-slug column would have produced five 404s, including on a page already flagged for
rewrite-level review. The lesson generalizes past this table: **DOI slugs are opaque identifiers,
not derived strings** — treat any future `x##`-style map as data to harvest, never to compute.

Chapter 20 breaks the pattern completely: Medication-Induced Movement Disorders has **no `x##`
prefix at all** (`.Medication_Induced_Movement_Disorders`), as does the front matter
(`.Introduction`, plus `.x00_Diagnostic_Classification` for the DSM-5-TR Classification). Neither
is taught, so this doesn't cost the course anything — but it's why the ordinal rule cannot be
trusted past 19.

**Proxy path confirmed by Norm, 2026-07-25** — that URL hits a UTORid login wall when

**These links are for readers, not for fetching.** An agent cannot verify them by retrieval —
PsychiatryOnline returns 403 to automated requests, and a signed-in *human* session does not

chapters through EZproxy is what library terms of use prohibit and what gets a UTORid suspended,
and it would contradict §2.1's central decision — the wiki *links* criteria and never carries them.
Verify slugs the way they were harvested (public search index); let the student's own browser do the
authenticated part.

## 5. Page types

Four kinds of page, and only two of them cost model time or review hours.

| Type | Count | Generated? | Review |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lecture page** (L1–L11) | 11 | No — hand-written | Instructor authors directly: objectives, the lecture's disorder links, Emotion Form prompts, quiz/case-study pointers. ~10 min each, no source bundle |
| **Topic overview** | 16 | Yes | The shared conceptual core the course teaches *as* a block (e.g. triple vulnerability for anxiety; tolerance/withdrawal/reinforcement for substances; clusters + FFM for personality). Full review |
| **Foundations page** | 9 | Yes | Non-disorder anchors the exams weight heavily (assessment, diagnosis, research methods, suicide, law, ethics) |
| **Disorder page — Tier A** | 53 | Yes | Full §2.4 seven-section treatment; full instructor review (~10–15 min) |
| **Disorder page — Tier B** | 45 | Yes, cheaply | Short page: name, class, one-paragraph description, **criteria deep-link**, differential pointers to its Tier A neighbours, empty student-contribution slot. ~3 min review |

> Counts revised 2026-07-30 (was 46/52): the seven Tier B personality disorders were promoted, so
> all ten PDs now get full pages. See §6 chapter 18. **The reader surfaces these labels to
> students** as "central to the course" / "supporting page", with the explicit caveat that a
> supporting page is shorter for review-budget reasons and not because the disorder is off the
> syllabus — the tier must never be read as "skippable".

**Why the two tiers.** Tier B is not filler — it is what keeps the link graph closed. A Tier A
page's *differential diagnosis* section names its neighbours (a panic disorder page has to
mention separation anxiety and selective mutism), and an unresolved wikilink is a dead end for a
student following it. A 150-word stub with a criteria link is a legitimate destination; a red
link is not.

**Tier B is also the natural student-contribution target.** A stub that a student expands from
their own paper, instructor-reviewed before publish, is exactly the graded artifact R3/R5 want.
So the recommended fall scope is: **generate all Tier A + all overviews + all foundations now
(71 pages); generate Tier B stubs as bare skeletons from the taxonomy seed with no source
bundle and no model call** (name + class + criteria DOI + differential pointers are all
mechanical from the seed), and let WP6's student flow thicken them during term.

That way nothing is a dead link on day one, and the 52 Tier B pages cost ~0 review hours in
August instead of ~2.5 hours of stub-reading.

   and `lecture` is what makes a lecture page's links generate themselves.
2. **A `lecture` entity is needed, not just a class overview page.** The plan assumed
   class overview = anchor page; §5 splits them, because the pedagogically useful lecture page
   (objectives + links + Emotion Form prompts + assignment pointers) is navigation over the
   DSM-chapter content, not a content page. Cheap: 11 hand-written pages, and it's the
   natural join point to Lecture Lounge.
3. **Two DSM chapters get no pages at all** and two get merged overviews (bipolar+depressive;
   gender dysphoria folded into its single disorder page) — record this in the seed so a later
   session doesn't "fix" the gap.
4. **The gender dysphoria page and the paraphilic overview need rewrite-level review, not
   approve-level.** The source deck's terminology is dated in the first case and the framing is
   deliberately provocative in the second (it works live, in a room, with a lecturer present;
   it does not work as unattended reference text). Budget 30 min each rather than 15.
5. **Appendix A is a Lecture Lounge deliverable, not a wiki one** — the 11 lectures' Emotion
   Form prompts are the check-in prompt library the classroom app needs, and the decks were

   data-model constraint: a prompt is anchored to a *section boundary within* a lecture, not to
   the lecture, so a one-check-in-per-session schema can't represent the library as run.

**Where they sit is part of the design.** Reading L1–L5 slide-by-slide makes the placement rule
visible: with one exception, each prompt is on the slide *immediately before* the block it hooks
into, so it is asked while the students' own answer is still the only material in the room. L3's
"something you get anxious about" opens the GAD block; its "can't stop thinking about" prompt
opens Ch 6; L4's "doing repeatedly" prompt opens the OCD recap, "how do you manage distress"
opens trauma, and "glitch in the matrix" opens dissociation; L5 runs body-felt depression →
Depressive Disorders, "so 'up' you weren't yourself" → Bipolar, "low vs. too much energy" →
Treatment. The exception is the *first* prompt of L2 and L3, which is a course-level check
("most confusing topic so far") rather than a content hook — and it is the one prompt reused
verbatim across two weeks.

Two consequences for the Lecture Lounge prompt library, which is the deliverable this appendix
feeds (§8.5):

- The unit is a **prompt anchored to a section boundary**, not a prompt-per-lecture. ~35 prompts
  across 11 lectures, 2–4 per lecture, each attached to the block that follows it. A data model
  with one check-in per session loses the thing that makes them work.
- **A prompt is reusable and some are lecture-agnostic.** "Most confusing topic so far" is a
  recurring course-level beat, and the polls ("Rate the Midterm!", "Are you dieting right now?")
  are a different type again — instrumented in-class polls, not reflective prompts. Worth
  distinguishing at least *hook* / *course check* / *poll* in whatever the library stores.

L1 is the only lecture whose break-slide prompts were never written down (the two "Break Time!"
slides carry the dashboard link alone); given the icebreaker is course-admin rather than content,
L1 likely ran with one prompt, and nothing needs recovering.

## Appendix B — why the disorder page structure is what it is

The **Case Study Report** (10% of the grade) asks students, for each of four vignettes, to:

1. list the relevant symptoms and signs observed,
2. rank them by importance as sources of dysfunction and suffering,
3. name the **2–3 most likely** disorders and the symptoms pointing to each,
4. give the **differential diagnosis criteria** between those 2–3 — the critical features that
   decide which fits,
5. pick the best fit and recommend a therapy type.

Plus: the weekly textbook case studies (another 10%), and L9's worked example (Rachel) which
also names the vignette's **red herrings** explicitly.

This is why §2.4's page template leads with *what it looks like* → *how it's diagnosed
(including differential diagnosis and specifiers)* → *what helps*: the page sections are the
assignment's steps, in order. Two implications worth carrying into WP3's prompt:

- **The differential-diagnosis section is the highest-value section on every page**, and it is
  the section that requires Tier B pages to exist as link targets.
- **Red herrings deserve a slot.** The instructor's own model answer distinguishes symptoms
  that point at the diagnosis from symptoms that plausibly mislead. A `contested` section
  already exists in the template; consider a distinct "commonly confused with" block, generated
  from the same neighbour set as the differential pointers.
