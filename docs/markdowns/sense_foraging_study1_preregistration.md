# Sense Foraging Scale, Study 1: exploratory factor analysis of item pool 6

OSF Preregistration (standard template). University of Toronto REB Protocol #00051180, Study 1 amendment approved 2026-10-11.

## 1. Study information

### Title

Development of the Sense Foraging Scale, Study 1: exploratory factor structure of a 48-item pool with reverse-keyed items

### Authors

Norman Farb (principal investigator), University of Toronto. Co-investigators as listed on REB Protocol #00051180.

### Description

The Sense Foraging Scale is a new self-report measure of how people move between two ways of paying attention: a sensing mode (taking in what is present through the senses, without needing to do anything with it) and a doing mode (using what one knows to plan, solve, judge, fix or get something done). It is intended as an outcome measure for brief training in moving into the sensing mode, including a 4-week course evaluated in a randomized trial.

Study 1 is the exploratory step. Adults recruited on Prolific completed a short background questionnaire and a 48-statement item pool (item pool 6), presented in a constrained random order. The pool contains 34 positively keyed items written for 11 hypothesized facets, grouped a priori into seven hypothesized factors, and 14 reverse-keyed items, each written as the logical opposite of a named positive item. The reverse-keyed items are included so that a general tendency to agree (acquiescence) can be separated from content. This matters because the course teaches the same "sensing mode / doing mode" vocabulary the items use, so on an all-positive scale a rise in agreement after the course could not be distinguished from real change.

The aims are to (1) estimate the factor structure of the pool while modelling acquiescence, and (2) select a shorter set of about 21–28 items by pre-specified rules. The short set will be confirmed in independent samples: the baseline of the course trial and Study 2.

### Hypotheses

Study 1 is exploratory. We state expectations, not confirmatory tests:

1. **Structure.** The positively keyed items will form several correlated factors resembling the seven hypothesized factors: Action, View, Openness, Reward / awe, Mode awareness, Noting without elaborating, and Stress and safety. The number of factors is decided by the procedure in Section 5, not assumed. The data may merge or split some of the seven.
2. **Acquiescence.** A random-intercept (acquiescence) factor will have non-zero variance.
3. **Reverse-keyed items.** Once acquiescence is modelled, most reverse-keyed items will load on the factor of their partner item rather than forming a separate factor made mainly of reversals. A factor defined mainly by reversals will be interpreted as a method factor, not a facet.
4. **Known groups (exploratory).** People currently practising meditation, yoga or a similar mind-body practice at least weekly will score higher than people who have never practised regularly, most clearly on Action, Mode awareness and Noting without elaborating.

## 2. Design plan

### Study type

Observational study: a single-session online survey.

### Blinding

No blinding is involved. Participants were not told the hypotheses or which items are reverse-keyed; the debrief explains both.

### Study design

The study is cross-sectional, with one session of about 10 minutes. Participants first answered 13 background questions: age, gender, race/ethnicity, country, education, contemplative-practice history (four follow-up questions shown only to those reporting regular practice), religiosity, spirituality and tradition. They then completed the 48 items and two instructed-response attention checks.

The response scale is 6 points, every point labelled: 1 Strongly disagree, 2 Disagree, 3 Mildly disagree, 4 Mildly agree, 5 Agree, 6 Strongly agree. There is no midpoint. Every item except the attention checks also offers "Prefer not to answer". Items were shown about 8 per page, on 6 pages of 9/9/8/8/8/8. The exact materials are in the supplementary file *Study1_Supplementary_Materials.docx*.

Recruitment used two Prolific postings that differ only in their title and description, as approved by the REB. One is framed as general interest ("Everyday Experience & Wellbeing Survey"). The other is framed as contemplative-leaning ("Mindfulness, Body Awareness & Everyday Experience Survey"). The aim is a broader spread of practice experience; nobody was screened on practice. Both postings lead to the identical study, and the posting is recorded per participant.

### Randomization

Item order was randomized per participant: a seeded, constrained random order, stable if the participant reloaded. Orders were drawn at random from all orders satisfying these rules:

- items of the same hypothesized factor at least 3 positions apart and at most 2 per page;
- five near-duplicate pairs (items 4–13, 16–18, 1–9, 6–16, 5–10) at least 5 positions apart;
- each reverse-keyed item on a different page from its partner;
- 2–3 reverse-keyed items per page, never adjacent to each other, never first, never beside an attention check;
- no more than 5 positively keyed items in a row;
- one attention check in each half of the questionnaire.

Each item's position is recorded, so order effects can be examined.

## 3. Sampling plan

### Existing data

**Registration prior to analysis of the data.** Data collection began on 2026-10-11 and was essentially complete when this registration was written the same day: 510 enrollments and 477 completed sessions across both postings. No factor-analytic or structural analysis has been run.

### Explanation of existing data

Before registration, the following were inspected, for quality control only:

- **Pilot (first 20 places of the general posting):**
  - completion counts;
  - attention-check pass rates (15/15 completers passed both);
  - completion time (median about 9 minutes);
  - "Prefer not to answer" counts;
  - the standard deviation and number of distinct responses per person (no straightlining).
- **Full sample:** enrollment and completion counts per posting, and the distribution of the practice-history questions (to judge whether the contemplative posting was needed).
- **One structural statistic (pilot, n = 15):** the correlation within each of the 14 reverse-keyed / partner pairs (range −.84 to +.32), computed as an early check that the reversals behave as opposites. No other inter-item correlations, factor solutions, loadings or reliabilities were computed on any data.

The analysis code will be developed on a copy of the data in which each item's responses are permuted across participants (preserving each item's distribution and destroying its correlations). It will be run on the real data only after this registration is filed.

### Data collection procedures

- **Participants:** Prolific participants aged 18+, fluent in English, resident in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Ireland or New Zealand. The contemplative posting excluded participants of the general posting. The platform also recognises a Prolific ID it has already enrolled and does not record a second participation.
- **Platform:** the RADlab research platform (radlab.zone). Prolific IDs are held in a separate table, replaced by a random code in all study data, and deleted after payment.
- **Compensation:** USD 12 per hour, prorated (about USD 2.00), paid regardless of answers, including the attention checks.
- **Pilot:** the first 20 places, run with the identical materials. Nothing was changed after the pilot, so pilot participants are included.

### Sample size

Target: 500 participants, 250 per posting (the pilot counts toward the general posting). Expected analysable N is about 470–480 after incomplete sessions and exclusions.

### Sample size rationale

- **Items per participant.** About 10 participants per item for 48 items.
- **Factor recovery depends on communalities and overdetermination.** With the moderate-to-low communalities typical of new self-report items, several hundred participants are needed for stable recovery even when factors have 6–7 items each (MacCallum, Widaman, Zhang & Hong, 1999). Our seven hypothesized factors have 4–9 items each, mean 6.9.
- **More factors and lower communalities raise the minimum N.** Required N climbs into the several hundreds for designs like this one (Mundfrom, Shaw & Ke, 2005). N = 500 is rated "very good" by the conventional benchmarks (Comrey & Lee, 1992).
- **The models need more data than a plain EFA.** Ordinal estimation on 6-category items with sparse extreme categories, plus a random-intercept acquiescence factor with reverse-keyed items, both do. Simulations of the random-intercept approach covered N = 200–1,000 (de la Fuente & Abad, 2020). We aimed at the upper part of that range.
- **Secondary.** With roughly half the sample practising and half not (as observed in the general posting), two groups of about 250 give 80% power to detect a difference of d ≈ 0.25 (two-tailed α = .05) in the exploratory known-groups comparison.
- **Practical constraint.** About 10 minutes per participant, paid at USD 12 per hour.

### Stopping rule

The number of places was fixed on Prolific in advance: 20 (pilot) + 230 on the general posting, and 250 on the contemplative posting. Data collection stops when these places are filled. No interim analysis informs stopping, and excluded participants are not replaced.

## 4. Variables

### Manipulated variables

None. Posting (general vs contemplative framing) is a recruitment condition, not a randomized manipulation. It is recorded from the Prolific study id and used as a covariate.

### Measured variables

- **Items:** the 48 pool-6 items (`sf6_1` to `sf6_48`, 1–6, or "Prefer not to answer"). Items 35–48 are reverse-keyed and stored unrecoded.
- **Attention checks:** `sf6_attn_disagree` (correct = 2) and `sf6_attn_agree` (correct = 5).
- **Background questions:** as in the supplementary file.
- **Paradata:**
  - each item's presented position;
  - total seconds on the scale and seconds per item;
  - posting.

### Indices

- **Factor scores and reliability:** factor scores from the final model; ordinal omega per factor and hierarchical omega.
- **Practice group:**
  - "current practitioner" = regular practice "Yes" and current frequency 1–2 times a week or more;
  - "never" = regular practice "No";
  - others (practised before, less than weekly now) are reported separately.
- **Inconsistency index:** the number of the 14 reverse-keyed / partner pairs answered on the same side of the scale (both 1–3, or both 4–6).
- **Longstring:** the longest run of identical answers in presented order.

## 5. Analysis plan

### Statistical models

R, using psych, lavaan, EFAtools and siren; versions are reported. Item scores are treated as ordinal (polychoric correlations, WLSMV estimation).

1. **Pre-checks.**
   - Item distributions, floor and ceiling, and "Prefer not to answer" rates.
   - Polychoric correlations among the near-duplicate pairs (4–13, 16–18, 1–9, 6–16, 5–10), screened for residual correlations before the acquiescence model is fitted. An acquiescence factor can absorb doublets.
2. **Number of factors.** Parallel analysis on the polychoric correlations of the keyed items, with reverse-keyed items recoded (principal-axis factors, 500 random datasets, 95th percentile). Models with k = PA − 1, PA and PA + 1 factors are estimated, together with the hypothesized 7-factor and 11-facet solutions.
   - The solution is chosen by these criteria, in order:
     1. every factor has at least 3 items with primary loadings ≥ .40;
     2. interpretability against the hypothesized factors;
     3. fit, reported descriptively (CFI, RMSEA, SRMR).
   - The choice and its reasons are documented.
3. **Primary model.** An exploratory structural equation model with:
   - the chosen number of correlated content factors (oblique, partially specified target rotation toward the hypothesized factor membership);
   - a random-intercept acquiescence factor: loadings fixed to 1 on all 48 unrecoded items, variance estimated, uncorrelated with the content factors (Maydeu-Olivares & Coffman, 2006; Aichholzer, 2014).
4. **Reliability.** Ordinal omega for each retained factor and hierarchical omega.
5. **Item selection for the short form.**
   - Retain 3–4 items per retained factor (target 21–28 items). Prefer items with:
     1. primary loading ≥ .50 (minimum .40);
     2. no secondary loading ≥ .30, and primary minus secondary ≥ .20;
     3. "Prefer not to answer" ≤ 5%;
     4. content coverage of the factor (at most one item of each near-duplicate pair).
   - Retain 4–6 reverse-keyed pairs in the short form, so acquiescence stays estimable in later samples. A pair qualifies when its polychoric correlation is ≤ −.30 and the reverse-keyed item's primary loading on its factor is ≥ .40 in the primary model.
   - The final selection is made by the PI applying these criteria; every departure from them is reported with its reason.
6. **Known groups (exploratory).** Current practitioners vs never-practitioners, on factor scores from the final model: linear models with posting as a covariate, Holm correction across factors, standardized differences reported. Posting differences are reported descriptively.

### Transformations

Reverse-keyed items are recoded (7 − x) for parallel analysis, reliability and scoring, and left unrecoded in models with the random-intercept factor. No other transformations.

### Inference criteria

The factor analyses are exploratory: decisions follow the criteria above, and fit indices are descriptive. The known-groups comparisons use α = .05 with Holm correction and are labelled exploratory.

### Data exclusion

- **Excluded:**
  - staff test enrollments;
  - sessions without a submitted scale;
  - participants who fail **both** attention checks.
- **Repeat submissions:** the participant's first complete submission of each questionnaire is used.
- **Flagged and examined in sensitivity analyses, not excluded:**
  - failing exactly one attention check;
  - fewer than 2 seconds per item on the scale;
  - a longstring of 15 or more;
  - an inconsistency index of 7 or more (of 14 pairs);
  - "Prefer not to answer" on more than 9 of the 48 items.

### Missing data

"Prefer not to answer" and unanswered items are treated as missing. Polychoric correlations and WLSMV use pairwise-available data. Items with more than 5% missing are reported. Factor scores use available items.

### Exploratory analysis

**Sensitivity analyses**, each compared with the primary solution:

1. EFA of the 34 positively keyed items only;
2. the primary model without flagged respondents;
3. an acquiescence-corrected EFA by the Lorenzo-Seva and Ferrando method (siren);
4. the primary model without the random-intercept factor.

**Further exploratory analyses:**

- the effect of presented position on item means and loadings;
- the inconsistency index by first language and posting;
- configural comparison of the factor structure across postings, if each posting supports it.

## 6. Other

**Next steps.** The short form selected here will be administered in a 4-week course trial (baseline from 2026-10-17), whose baseline data provide an independent sample for a confirmatory factor analysis, and in Study 2. Study 2 is a new Prolific sample that adds established measures of mindfulness, interoceptive awareness, wellbeing and socially desirable responding.

**Data sharing.** A de-identified dataset will be shared as described in the consent form: no exact timestamps or durations, age in bands, coarsened rare demographic combinations, free text recoded. The analysis code will be shared alongside it. The background and evidence for the reverse-keyed design are summarized in the supplementary file.
