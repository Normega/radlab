## Debrief form

### About This Study

**Approved:**

> This study is testing a new questionnaire designed to measure people's ability to notice and shift into a receptive, sensory way of engaging with the world (sometimes called "sense foraging"), as distinct from a more goal-directed, evaluative way of engaging with it. We are examining whether this new questionnaire has a coherent factor structure and how it relates to established measures of trait mindfulness (FFMQ-15) and interoceptive (bodily) awareness (MAIA-2).

**Study 2:**

> This study is testing a new questionnaire designed to measure people's ability to notice and shift into a receptive, sensory way of engaging with the world (sometimes called "sense foraging"), as distinct from a more goal-directed, evaluative way of engaging with it. In an earlier study its statements formed four parts: getting into the sensing mode, understanding the two modes, valuing and savouring time spent sensing, and letting experience be. This study checks that structure in a new group of people and examines how the questionnaire relates to established measures of trait mindfulness (FFMQ-15), interoceptive (bodily) awareness (MAIA-2), decentering (Experiences Questionnaire), intolerance of uncertainty (IUS-12), savouring (Savoring Beliefs Inventory) and openness to experience (BFI-2). Twenty-four further statements are ones we are testing for a future version, and some statements were worded in the opposite direction from others, which helps us separate what people agree with from a general tendency to agree.

### About This Study (second paragraph)

**Approved:**

> We are also comparing responses between people who regularly engage in a contemplative practice (such as meditation or yoga) and those who do not, to see whether practitioners score differently on this new measure, as a way of testing whether it captures something meaningful. Finally, we included two brief wellbeing measures (a life satisfaction item and the PHQ-4 mood/anxiety screener) to explore, on an exploratory basis, whether the capacity for receptive sensory engagement relates to general wellbeing.

**Study 2:**

> We are also comparing responses between people who regularly engage in a contemplative practice (such as meditation or yoga) and those who do not, to see whether practitioners score differently on this new measure, as a way of testing whether it captures something meaningful. Finally, we included brief wellbeing and stress measures (a life satisfaction item, the PHQ-4 mood/anxiety screener and the Perceived Stress Scale) and a few questions about your past week, to explore whether the capacity for receptive sensory engagement relates to general wellbeing and everyday behaviour. Two statements asked you to select a specific answer; these help us check that statements were read carefully.
